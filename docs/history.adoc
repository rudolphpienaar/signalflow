== Abstract

SignalFlow began on February 27, 2026 as a side quest: a small but serious attempt to trace AI-assisted code flow by compiling static source into an ASCII-geometric representation that could give a human an immediate gestalt insight into execution shape. The starting intuition was simple. If code could be turned into chips, ports, and wires, then structural bottlenecks would become visible immediately. A function called from many modules and calling many downstream children would stop being an abstract graph statistic and become a geometric fact.

That modest objective did not remain modest. The project quickly evolved from a topological sketcher into a far more demanding investigation of geometric chip layout, module grouping, internal wiring, boundary piercing, zone-local routing, seam materialization, inter-zone geometry, debugger surfaces, and finally re-architected routing substrates. Along the way, SignalFlow ceased to be just a renderer. It became a deterministic apparatus for exposing where AI-assisted engineering breaks down.

This is the deeper drama of the project. The more rigorously the system was pushed toward explicit ownership, declarative geometry, and deterministic rendering, the more glaring the limitations of current AI tooling became. Claude could spin for twenty minutes on trivial architectural asks. Codex could read rendered ASCII and still fail to infer obvious geometric consequences unless the user manually restated the invariants. SignalFlow therefore became both the thing being built and the instrument by which the weaknesses of AI-mediated architecture could be measured.

That dual role defines the project. SignalFlow is not merely a visualization engine. It is a deterministic compilation-and-rendering substrate whose very correctness pressures expose the hallucinations, drift, weak spatial reasoning, and pseudo-progress tendencies of contemporary AI systems. This document records that evolution as a narrative and opinionated technical history, from the first commit through the current `v6.0.7` working line.

== The Meta-Method: Human Architect, LLMs, and a Deterministic World

Before the historical phases begin, one fact should be stated plainly. SignalFlow is not merely a software project built with AI assistance. It is also a running inquiry into what AI assistance can and cannot do when the target system depends on a coherent internal world.

This distinction is essential. SignalFlow is not a presentation-first artifact. The rendering is only the visible surface of a deeper geometric order with its own internal laws of structure, continuity, adjacency, and consequence. The architecture flows from that order outward, not the other way around. The domain-specific objects of SignalFlow, such as chip walls, fan regions, seam lanes, and routing boundaries, matter only because they are concrete manifestations of that deeper world and its physics.

This has proven to be a particularly clean failure region for current LLMs. They are consistently drawn toward the presentation layer. They can often propose plausible-looking repairs to the picture while failing to reason from the world model that makes the picture correct. In that sense, SignalFlow is almost too perfect a test. It is a geometric presentation of an underlying internal physics. Current LLMs can often speak about the surface while failing to preserve the physics.

The broader literature is not identical in language, but it is directionally consistent with this experience. Some work argues that text-only systems cannot acquire grounded meaning from form alone in the human sense, and that language understanding ultimately requires relation to the world and to experience (Bender and Koller, 2020; Bisk et al., 2020). Other work finds evidence that LLMs do learn partial internal representations of space and time (Gurnee and Tegmark, 2024). The correct conclusion here is neither mysticism nor denial. It is simply that whatever partial world-like regularities these systems acquire are not the same thing as the disciplined, explicit internal model demanded by a project like SignalFlow. That gap becomes especially obvious when the task requires reasoning about action, change, consequence, and structural consistency rather than local verbal plausibility (Valmeekam et al., 2023).

This is why the project required irreducible human architectural labor. The core semantics, the geometric truths, the routing algebra, the ownership rules, the attach-point doctrine, the distinction between render overlays and geometry owners, and the eventual re-architecture from chip-local manifold logic into a routed world substrate all had to be built by hand. The LLMs did not discover these truths. At best, they sometimes helped implement them after they had already been invented.

And even then, they often still failed.

=== The REPL as Epistemic Instrument

That failure created a practical methodological problem. Once the project had acquired a genuine internal world, it was no longer enough to stare at final renders and argue about them. A terminal rendering is evidence, but it is late evidence. By the time a wire looks wrong, many deeper questions are already in play:

- which geometric owner claimed the space
- which route family was chosen
- which lane identity was assigned
- which boundary was treated as real
- and whether the bug lives in placement, solve, realization, or composition

Without a way to interrogate those layers directly, development collapses into an exhausting cycle of aesthetic argument and guesswork. Worse, that is exactly the environment in which LLMs sound confident while remaining structurally wrong. They can describe the artifact, but they cannot expose the hidden commitments that produced it.

The project therefore needed an intermediate surface between code and final render: a place where the internal world could be queried directly. That is what the REPL and debugger became. They were not conceived as a luxury shell or a nice operator convenience. They became the point at which the engine could be asked what it actually believed.

This is why the REPL mattered so much. It made it possible to inspect the world at multiple semantic depths rather than only at the final composed picture. The human architect could ask for owned geometry, actual rendered crops, route sets, interconnect frames, placement bands, attach points, and debug handles for specific zones or chips. That changed the entire development method, because it turned disputes about the system from vague interpretation into direct interrogation.

In concrete terms, the REPL made it possible to:

- inspect owned zone regions
- inspect interconnect frames
- inspect local and seam routes
- inspect world crops
- compare geometry ownership with final composition

This in turn shaped the REPL itself. It stopped being merely a front-end for convenience commands and became a shared semantic surface between the architecture and the AI assistant. It provided a place where internal world state could be made explicit enough that incorrect AI reasoning could be exposed, corrected, and often disproven by direct inspection. In that sense, the REPL belongs to the methodological history of the project, not merely to its tooling history.

SignalFlow should therefore be read as having two simultaneous subjects:

- the construction of a deterministic geometry engine for code-flow visualization
- the discovery, under pressure, of the limits of AI-assisted architecture when correctness depends on a coherent internal world model

== Lineage and Prior Art

SignalFlow did not emerge in a vacuum. It sits near several adjacent traditions, but it does not coincide with any of them.

It is similar to UML Sequence Diagrams in that both try to explain software interaction. But UML is fundamentally temporal and message-oriented. It spends its vertical axis on time and treats calls as discrete arrows between lifelines. SignalFlow instead treats a call thread as routed continuity in space. It is topological before it is temporal.

It is similar to Flame Graphs in that both can reveal concentration and bottlenecks. But Flame Graphs are statistical and performance-oriented. They answer where time was spent. SignalFlow instead tries to answer where structure lives, how modules couple, and how the call/return substrate itself is shaped.

It is similar to ordinary flowcharts in that both depict logic flow. But flowcharts tend to privilege branching control syntax and symbolic decision nodes. SignalFlow is not trying to restate source code syntax. It is trying to materialize a routed architectural substrate.

Most importantly, it is directly similar to classic Signal Flow Graphs in systems and control engineering. That lineage is not an analogy pasted on afterward; it is one of the conceptual roots of the project. SignalFlow borrows the idea that a system can be understood as a directed flow substrate whose continuity matters mathematically, not merely pictorially.

The differences matter just as much. Traditional signal flow graphs usually speak in terms of gains, reductions, and formal system response. SignalFlow instead uses the same spirit of continuity to materialize software calls, returns, module boundaries, and attachment semantics. It applies a signal-graph worldview to code structure rather than to transfer-function algebra.

== Engineering Lineage and Personal Motive

The project is also entirely on brand at a personal level. Coming from an electronic and computer engineering background, the idea that code should be visualizable as actual circuitry is not a gimmick but an almost natural reflex. Chips, signals, returns, routing lanes, fan-in/fan-out, trunks, and interconnects are not imported metaphors. They are the native vocabulary by which structural truth becomes legible.

This is also where the influence of Signal Flow Graphs is strongest. The ability to show the logical or mathematical representation of a system as direct signal flow was an early and durable inspiration for the project. SignalFlow applies that instinct to software. The call is treated as a material departure. The explicit return is treated as a material arrival. The insistence on rendering both call and return as separate, visible structure is not a stylistic flourish. It is the core language by which continuity is preserved.

That call-and-return materialization rule is central. Without it, the graph becomes a half-truth. It may show that something invoked something else, but it does not show how continuity is preserved, how context returns, or where structural pressure accumulates. SignalFlow's explicit return path is therefore one of its defining differentiators.

== SignalFlow and the DAG Question

SignalFlow is related to DAGs, but it is not reducible to a DAG.

At one level, the project absolutely intersects with DAG thinking. Static source can be compiled into a canonical graph of chips and edges. The current engine explicitly works from canonical chips, explicit calls, short references, recursion edges, and ancestor or back edges. In that sense, SignalFlow needs graph structure in order to exist at all.

But code is not always a DAG in the strict sense. Functions can call themselves recursively. They can call earlier functions. They can participate in cycles of state mutation that govern subsequent execution decisions. Once those realities enter the picture, a pure DAG description becomes insufficient. A DAG can approximate or slice aspects of the system, but it cannot fully express the substrate.

This is why SignalFlow should be read as a static presentation of the whole callable graph as it relates to an execution substrate, not as a full execution simulator. The diagrams can show topological possibility, ownership, continuity, bottlenecks, and routed structure. They do not, by themselves, fully encode runtime state transitions, dynamic branch selection, loop counts, or temporal evolution.

To express that fully would require additional dimensions:

- time
- state
- execution selection
- and likely animation or staged reveal

So the correct claim is narrower and stronger at the same time. SignalFlow is not "the execution" itself. It is a deterministic static substrate that materially represents the callable structure and its routed continuity. It is execution-related, but not execution-complete.

== Phase I: The First Wager: Topology Over Ornament (v1.0.0 - v1.4.0)
_Period: February 27 – March 2, 2026_

=== Status Quo: The Side Quest

SignalFlow did not begin as a full systems program. It began as a question: could static code be transformed into a geometric representation rich enough to give a developer a faster "a-ha" than text, ASTs, or ordinary call graphs? The motivating image was chip-like. A function would become a unit with signal and return structure. Calls would become routed threads. High fan-in and high fan-out would become visible bottlenecks. The wager was that geometry could make software structure legible at a glance.

The early pressure was therefore not architectural purity. It was representational fidelity. If the picture failed to preserve thread continuity, chip identity, and port meaning, then the entire side quest would collapse into decorative ASCII. This was the first wager of the project: that geometry could carry structural truth rather than merely decorate it.

=== The Crossing: From Thought Experiment to Falsifiable Artifact

The initial release on February 27, 2026 proved the bet was worth making. The project crossed from idea into artifact, and that crossing mattered more than any feature the first release contained. SignalFlow acquired a falsifiable output surface. After that point, every bug and every architectural claim could be grounded in a visible failure rather than in taste or intuition. The project ceased to be a thought experiment and became a system that could be wrong in public — and one that would be wrong in public, repeatedly, in ways that forced it to become honest.

The consequences of that crossing appeared almost immediately. The first routing heuristics — the Double Helix and Descending Stagger algorithms in `v1.1.0` — revealed that call rendering is not merely about connectivity. It is about choreography. Signals and returns dumped arbitrarily onto a canvas do not produce a topology. They produce noise. The discovery was uncomfortable precisely because it was simple: if lane separation was not disciplined, the picture stopped being readable, and a picture that stopped being readable had already failed the project's core claim. Readability turned out not to be a post-processing concern. It was a routing concern from the very beginning.

=== The First Doctrinal Move: Ownership Before Expressiveness

The pattern that would define SignalFlow's entire subsequent arc announced itself in `v1.2.0`. The project introduced a structured, port-based schema for signal mapping and discovered that this small abstraction change had large downstream consequences. Once ports existed as first-class architectural objects, wires ceased to be generic adjacency between approximate regions. They became typed obligations between specific attachment sites. A call no longer connected "somewhere" on a chip. It connected through a defined port contract.

This was the first time the project chose ownership over expressiveness, and the choice proved correct in exactly the way such choices usually prove correct: not by producing a visible improvement in the next render, but by making a whole class of future bugs structurally impossible. Port semantics hardened the renderer against hand-wavy interpretation. The lesson was not yet fully stated, but the method was already implied. A representational system that permits vagueness in its core contracts will accumulate debt wherever that vagueness is exercised. Every subsequent architectural crisis in the project's history can be traced to a place where that principle was either forgotten or not yet understood.

=== Trace Synthesis and the End of the Side Quest

By `v1.3.0` and `v1.4.0`, the project had moved from heuristic sketching to explicit graph-theoretic reasoning and arrived at the Trace Synthesis framing: a methodology for preserving execution lineage through a deterministic visual grammar. The side quest no longer looked small. What had begun as a geometric curiosity was already demanding formal routing semantics, deterministic trace preservation, and a representational contract richer than any ordinary diagramming tool carried. The doctrinal shift of Phase I was blunt: SignalFlow would not be allowed to survive as a clever picture. It would either become a truthful topology engine or fail. That framing would apply with increasing pressure to every phase that followed.

== Phase II: The Manifold Expansion and Chip-Local Maximalism (v2.0.0 - v5.1.2)
_Period: March 2 – March 9, 2026_

=== Status Quo: The Renderer Wants to Become a Chip

Once chips and ports were taken seriously, the next pressure was inevitable: the project could not stop at inter-chip edges. If a chip was supposed to explain software structure, then the chip interior itself had to become architecturally meaningful. This is where SignalFlow stopped behaving like a diagram generator and started behaving like a tiny ASCII VLSI system. External wires alone could not explain code bottlenecks or thread continuity. The interior of the chip had to show how signals entered, traversed, crossed, reused manifolds, and exited — and it had to show that as geometry, not ornament. Phase II therefore became a period of chip-local maximalism: if more local structure could be made explicit, then more architectural truth could be made visible.

=== Algebra Before Ornament

`v2.0.0` is where the project first took its own glyph language seriously as a formal algebra. The Geometric Terminator Algebra and the Vector-Aware Intent Engine were not feature additions. They were the recognition that line endings, directional semantics, and boundary glyphs could not remain approximate. Once line behavior was ambiguous, every downstream inference became suspect. A renderer that treated its own visual vocabulary as ad hoc ornament was making itself unreadable from the inside. The project therefore committed to treating glyphs as a true contract — something that meant the same thing every time it appeared and could be derived rather than guessed.

`v2.1.0` extended that commitment to the chip interior in the most demanding way possible: high-resolution zoned manifolds and exhaustive piercing algebra. Chip interiors ceased to be decorative pass-through and became a routing problem with zones, trunks, anchor points, and controlled boundary crossings. This was the moment internal wiring stopped being a detail and became one of the core engines of the project. The chips were now routed spaces.

=== The Manifold Era and Its Lesson

The `v3.x` releases were the period in which that ambition had to prove itself as real architecture rather than aspiration. Dedicated trunk zones, top/bottom ribbon segregation, label-aware longitude channels, structured junction buses, and compact chip configurations all emerged here. But each increase in expressiveness widened the surface on which the geometry could drift. The result was a long chain of corrections — repeated repairs to offset formulas, repeated fixes to degenerate junction behavior, repeated audits of `chip_h_precompute` — and a lesson the project had to learn through accumulation rather than a single breakthrough: once geometry is the truth surface, there is no such thing as a small rendering bug. Every misplaced offset is an architectural error. Every plausible-looking fix that does not address the geometric root is debt.

This is also where AI assistance first showed its characteristic failure mode with unmistakable clarity. The manifold's richness created exactly the environment in which an LLM could be shown a rendered output, propose a plausible local repair, and produce a change that satisfied the visible artifact while corrupting the geometry that generated it. The manifold was complex enough that local plausibility was easy to achieve and global correctness was hard to verify without deep inspection. A language model working from visual evidence alone could not reliably distinguish between "the offset is wrong" and "the offset formula is wrong" — between fixing the instance and fixing the rule. The human architect had to carry that distinction, find the root, and repair it by hand. The project was already learning that AI assistance was most tempting and least trustworthy precisely at the boundary between visible surface and invisible invariant.

=== Consolidation and the Shape of the Ceiling

The `v4.x` line was the first serious stabilization. The repeated-child binding problem forced a more disciplined port identity model, culminating in `PortKey` replacing raw identity hacks. Configuration layering matured. Architecture documents, limitations catalogues, and a coherent glyph vocabulary gave the project an intellectual surface that could be referred to rather than reconstructed from renders. This era proved the first engine could be documented and stabilized to a serious degree. But stability here was local, not final. The engine was getting denser and smarter inside the chip while remaining conceptually empty outside it. The manifold model was powerful precisely where it was confined, and the confinement was exactly where the ceiling lived.

`v5.0.0` and `v5.1.x` pushed internal wiring to its highest expression: explicit orientation, endpoint-aware routing, module-aware layout, recursion-safe traversal. This was the high-water mark of the first engine. It was also the moment the ceiling became undeniable. The project could now make chip interiors richly expressive and locally deterministic, but it was simultaneously being forced to answer questions the first engine had no vocabulary for. How should routing behave when it leaves a chip? How should adjacent chip fields relate when the structure is no longer purely local? How should cross-chip obligations be represented as geometry rather than as stitched heuristics? The first engine had no answers — not because it was poorly built, but because it was built around the wrong unit of truth. It was chip-local. The world was not.

== Phase III: The Ceiling: Chip-Local Intelligence Is Not Enough (March 16, 2026 - v5.2.0)
_Period: March 16 – March 19, 2026_

=== Status Quo: The Manifold Engine Runs Out of Road

The diagnosis came slowly, but it was not wrong. By mid-March the legacy engine had become simultaneously impressive and boxed in. The architectural problem was no longer how to draw a beautiful manifold. It was how to reason cleanly about routing outside and between chips without perpetually bolting more special cases onto a chip-local renderer — and that question had no good answer within the first engine's architecture because the first engine had no coherent model of the space outside chips. It had local geometry and local routing rules. It had no world.

The false doctrine that had to die was that enough chip-local cleverness could substitute for a coherent external routing world. The project had been testing that doctrine against harder and harder cases for several phases. Phase III is where it finally failed the test.

=== The Quarantine: Making the Break Honest

The move that made the re-architecture intellectually honest was the quarantine of the old implementation under `signalflow.legacy`. This was not cosmetic namespace gardening, and it should not be read as a routine cleanup. It was a commitment with asymmetric obligations: the legacy code would not be modified, only referenced; the new package root would be built from scratch. Without that asymmetry, every subsequent redesign would have been haunted by compatibility shortcuts. The temptation to patch the old engine rather than replace its concepts is always present, and it is always the wrong choice when the concepts are the problem. Creating a hard boundary between the two codebases was the only way to ensure that the new architecture could be built from first principles rather than from accumulated debt.

This step also had a methodological consequence for AI collaboration. An LLM working in a mixed legacy/new codebase will pattern-match toward whatever has the most surface area. Legacy code that has been in the repository longest tends to set the pattern. By quarantining the legacy engine, the project created a space where the new architecture could establish its own patterns before they were contaminated by the old ones. The quarantine was as much about directing AI assistance correctly as it was about code organization.

=== The World as the Unit of Truth

What arrived immediately after the quarantine was not a refactored chip renderer. It was a different kind of object: routing zones, routing zone grids, interconnects, assignment sets, and explicit obligation and solve stages. These objects named the world as the unit of analysis rather than the chip. A `RoutingZone` does not describe a chip interior. It describes a named region of the world with owned geometry, explicit routing families, and an assignment contract that governs which chips belong in it. A `RoutingZoneGrid` does not describe a collection of chips. It describes the spatial structure of the world itself.

This distinction is easy to understate and essential not to. The zone-grid foundation was not a refactor. It was the recognition that routing should be expressed as ownership over geometric regions of the world, and that once that ownership was made explicit, the project's center of gravity changed immediately and permanently. Geometry was no longer a property of chips alone. It was a property of the world. The chip was now one object inside the world, not the world's organizing principle.

=== The v5.2.0 Cut

The first serious zone geometry and routing machinery arrived rapidly in the days following the quarantine. Crossbar corridor refinement, span confinement, corner-cell ownership, the zone-local clockwise concentric-rectangle solver, module boxes, wire-wall piercing, and return seam routes all emerged in close succession. None of these individually constitutes the meaning of `v5.2.0`. The meaning of the cut is the shift it represents: SignalFlow ceased to be primarily a chip-manifold renderer and became a routing substrate with explicit world geometry. That is a statement about what the engine believes is true, not about which features it supports. The conceptual center of gravity moved from "how to draw a chip interior" to "how to own and solve routed space," and that move does not appear in any individual commit. It appears in the fact that after the cut, the engine's primary noun was no longer the chip. It was the world.

== Phase IV: World Physics, Debugger Truth, and AI Exposure (v5.2.0 - v5.5.0+)
_Period: March 19, 2026 – present_

=== Status Quo: Geometry Becomes Doctrine

Once the zone-grid architecture existed, the project entered its current and most revealing phase. The work stopped being about adding raw capability and became about forcing every routing claim to cash out into deterministic geometry. This is where SignalFlow most clearly became an instrument for exposing AI architectural weakness.

The reason is straightforward. A deterministic geometry engine is merciless. A seam either preserves lane identity or it does not. A fan region either sits adjacent to the actual chip wall or it does not. A module box either acts as a render-only overlay or it has illicitly contaminated geometry ownership. There is very little room for performative understanding.

This is exactly where AI systems performed badly. They could often propose plausible language, but they routinely failed to derive the actual geometric consequence of visible rendered artifacts. The project therefore became an accidental but potent benchmark for AI architectural reasoning.

=== Versions 5.2.0 - 5.5.0: Seam Materialization, Module Semantics, and Debug Surfaces

The line from `v5.2.0` to `v5.5.0` was a sustained campaign to make the new engine honest about its own contracts. Seam routing moved toward per-directed-wire materialization so that individual wire identities survived their zone crossings rather than being absorbed into anonymous seam bundles. Module rendering semantics were clarified to enforce the rule that module boxes were render-only overlays rather than geometry owners — a distinction that sounds administrative but is architecturally decisive, because any object that owns geometry also owns routing space, and module boxes must not own routing space. World composition order was corrected so that the compositor worked in the right sequence: chip bodies, then module boxes, then routes. Each of these was a tightening of a contract, not an addition of a feature, and the distinction matters because contracts that are merely tacit get violated the moment an LLM proposes a change it understands only locally.

The debugger became an architectural instrument rather than a convenience shell during this same period. It became possible to ask the system for a zone's owned geometry, for the zone as the world actually draws it, for an interconnect frame in isolation, or for exact region names and positions. That capability changed the entire development method. Instead of arguing abstractly about what the engine should be doing, the geometry could be interrogated directly. Disputes that had previously required long prose reasoning sessions could now be resolved by running a single query and reading what the engine actually believed. This is where debugger truth replaced renderer impression as the decisive source of evidence — and where AI collaboration, for the first time, became tractable rather than merely probabilistic. A claim about geometry that contradicted a direct query result was not a matter of interpretation. It was simply wrong.

=== The AI-Failure Theme Becomes Explicit

This is the phase where the project's second subject became impossible to ignore. SignalFlow had begun as a way to trace AI-assisted code. It had become a way to expose how poorly current AI systems handle architecture when the system under discussion is deterministic, geometric, and relentlessly inspectable. The failure modes were consistent and structural, not accidental. A model presented with a rendered zone could describe it verbally with apparent confidence while failing to derive the geometric consequence that the render made obvious to any human with a spatial model of the system. The confusion between region ownership and rendered overlay was not a knowledge gap that more context could fill. It was evidence of a deeper problem: the models did not have a coherent internal world to reason from. They had a surface they could describe and a set of patterns they could match.

This distinction is the heart of the project's second subject. Pseudo-progress through abstraction cleanup, overfitting to brittle test snapshots, long-duration spinning on conceptually simple geometric tasks — these are not performance failures on hard problems. They are failures of a specific kind of structural coherence: the ability to hold a consistent internal model of a system and reason from its consequences rather than from the plausibility of local descriptions. SignalFlow, by demanding exactly that kind of coherence at every layer, became an almost perfectly calibrated instrument for exposing when that coherence was absent. A model can sound right while being geometrically wrong. SignalFlow makes that wrongness visible. That is not a side note to the project's history. It is now one of the main reasons the project exists.

=== Where the Project Stood at v5.5.0

At `v5.5.0`, SignalFlow was best understood as a living re-architecture rather than a finished renderer. It had moved from side quest to chip-manifold engine to zone-grid substrate. It had built a debugger that gave direct access to internal world state. It had established the rule that if the world model was wrong, no amount of render polish could save it. That was the stable framing at the end of Phase IV — but it was not the end of the story, because the world model itself was still incomplete. Zones existed. The world grid existed. But what happened at the boundary between two adjacent zones — where their extra rings collided, where their geometry had to resolve without a physics model to govern the resolution — had no answer yet. That answer required two more phases to arrive.

== Phase V: The Board-Era Re-Architecture and the Clean-Room Break (v5.5.0 - v5.9.9)
_Period: Late March 2026_

=== Status Quo: The Pattern-Matching Edge

The zone-grid engine introduced in Phase IV was a genuine architectural advance. It moved SignalFlow off chip-local maximalism and toward explicit routed-space ownership. But it had a structural flaw that grew harder to ignore as the routing surface expanded. The geometry it produced was not derived from first principles. It was assembled from a pile of placement heuristics, hardcoded default spans, and solver logic that lived in module-level constants scattered across several files with no canonical owner. No single object knew what a zone actually was in geometric terms. There was no physics. There was pattern-matching close to the answer, and then a collection of special cases beyond the edge of the pattern.

This became acute the moment routing had to reason about consequences rather than about local appearances. A zone's fan span, its terminal width, its longitude corridor — these values were being computed by different functions in different files from different sources with no coherent relationship to each other or to what the circuit actually demanded. An AI coding assistant working in this environment could plausibly patch one surface while quietly breaking three others it never examined. And it did, repeatedly. The project acquired a shorthand for this failure mode: doing a Gemini. A Gemini was a broad rewrite that pattern-matched its way to superficially passing tests while destroying underlying geometric integrity. It looked like progress and was in fact regression of the most expensive kind, because the regression was silent until a later rendering contradiction made it visible.

=== The Clean-Room Decision

The architectural response was unambiguous. The old routing machinery could not be patched toward correctness. It had to be quarantined more completely than the Phase III move had achieved, and a new runtime had to be built clean from the inside out, starting from objects that knew what they were and owned what they claimed. The new objects were not rewrites of old ones. They were fresh statements of the relevant geometry domain.

The center of the new runtime was a four-layer solve chain: `BoardChip` → `BoardKernel` → `Board` → `BoardSolver` → `BoardSolution` → `BoardMaterializedSolution`. Each layer was an honest owner of a specific phase of knowledge. `BoardKernel` knew the logical routing structure of a zone. `Board` knew its realized geometry. `BoardSolver` built an algebraic route plan from that geometry. `BoardSolution` held the symbolic result. `BoardMaterializedSolution` held the final world-coordinate output. No layer reached across this chain to inspect or mutate a sibling layer's state. The chain was the doctrine.

This matters beyond engineering tidiness. The reason the old engine was fragile is that its implicit contracts could be silently violated by any function that happened to know enough about internal structure to reach across them. The new chain made violation impossible by design. There was nothing to reach across, because the abstraction boundary was also the physical boundary of the object.

=== Truth Surfaces: REPL, Snippets, and SignalFlowContext

The other critical move in this phase was the elevation of the REPL and snippet infrastructure from convenience tooling to architectural truth surfaces. The `SignalFlowContext` object — originally introduced under the deeply unflattering name `NewEngineDebugContext`, a name that accurately captured how tentatively it had been conceived — became the normalized API entry point to the entire board-era world. The rename mattered because it was a commitment: this is not a debug convenience that might be removed. This is the surface through which the world is accessed.

Snippets acquired injected context, injected source paths, CLI passthrough arguments, and a principled `source_yaml` naming convention. They stopped being casual scratch files and became executable truth references that any agent or engineer could run against a canonical example and get reproducible world state back. The project's AI collaboration method changed substantially as a result. Disputes about geometry stopped being resolved by prose argument and started being resolved by running a snippet and reading what the engine actually believed. That change is one of the deeper methodological gains of the phase. It made hallucination expensive in a new way: a claim about geometry that contradicted snippet output was not a matter of interpretation. It was simply wrong.

=== The `boardGeometryConfig` Singleton and the Single Source of Truth

The geometry centralization arc completed this phase's main doctrinal work. The scattered module-level constants that had been the technical root of the pattern-matching brittleness were replaced by a coherent set of objects with clear ownership. `BoardGeometryConfig` became the process-level singleton loaded at CLI startup from XDG user configuration and project-level YAML. `RingGeometrySpec` and `BoardGeometrySpec` became the authoritative carriers of all zone geometry span knobs, both intra and extra. `ZoneSymbolicInvariants` became the site where circuit-derived constraints — wire demand, label lengths, placement-lifted region minimums — were computed once and made available to the spec through a single `with_invariants()` call.

The rule that emerged from this work was blunt: if a geometry value has no owner, it is not a geometry value. It is a drift hazard. Every span that had previously been implicit, defaulted in-place, or derived from ad hoc heuristics was assigned to one of these objects and one of these objects alone. The solver in `routing/placement.py` lost its module-level `_FAN_IN_OUT_SPAN` and `_TERMINAL_SPAN` constants and started reading live from `boardGeometryConfig` at call time. The system was no longer a collection of local estimates converging toward geometry. It was a geometry descending from a single declared truth.

== Phase VI: Zone-Overlap Physics and the World-Scale Routing Arc (v5.9.10 - v5.9.34)
_Period: April 2026_

=== Status Quo: The Seam Is the Problem

The board-era runtime solved the single-zone routing problem cleanly. Given one zone and one circuit, the geometry was deterministic, the solve was explicit, and the output was inspectable at every layer. But the world is not one zone. The world is a grid of zones, and when two zones are placed adjacent on that grid, they immediately reveal a problem that the single-zone model had no language to express.

Each zone carries an `extra` perimeter ring — a set of routing corridors that live just outside the intra substrate and provide the attachment surface for cross-zone communication. When zone (1,1) and zone (1,2) are placed touching on the world grid, their shared face is initially coincident: zone (1,1)'s east extra ring and zone (1,2)'s west extra ring both claim the same column space. There is no resolution rule, no geometry, and no physics to decide what happens. The seam is undefined. This is the hard edge of the problem space: not a rendering bug, not a solver failure, but a missing physical model.

=== The Geometry of Coincidence and the Perturbation Doctrine

The response to this gap was not a heuristic patch. It was a doctrine. The first question was how to think about zone adjacency correctly, and the answer that emerged from close examination of the geometry was that zones do not shift as rigid bodies. Their geometry is *perturbed at seams*. The extra ring of zone (1,1) and the extra ring of zone (1,2) each claim independent space at the shared face, and the minimum gap required to accommodate them both is the sum of their facing spans: stem:[\delta^{\text{east}} = N_{i,j} + M_{i,j+1}]. That gap is carved by pushing zone (1,2)'s geometry east. Not the zone as a logical entity, but its physical geometry from the collision point outward.

The directional constraint followed immediately from first principles. The world grid has two immovable anchors: the west origin column and the north origin row. All perturbation pressure must resolve away from those anchors — east for longitude-axis collisions, south for latitude-axis collisions. There is no such thing as a negative perturbation. This is not a policy choice. It is a consequence of fixing an origin.

The deeper realization was that this constraint made the world layout an a priori problem, not an iterative one. The seam deltas depend only on the extra ring spans of adjacent zones. Those spans come from `BoardGeometrySpec` and are known before any geometry is placed. There is no feedback loop: applying a perturbation does not change any zone's extra ring spans. The world layout is therefore a single-pass prefix sum over the zone grid — deterministic, O(n), and requiring no convergence criterion.

=== The `extra` Ring Architecture

The practical architecture that materialized around this doctrine was the full extra ring system. Four extra region families — `xwLong`, `xnLat`, `xeLong`, `xsLat` — are now live in the board geometry for WTE and ETW kernels. Transfer regions at the four corners where intra longitude bands meet extra latitude bands provide the explicit crossing points. An additional extra fan family (`xwFan`, `xeFan`) sits between the extra long channels and the chip terminal wall, giving the perimeter the same fan-in/fan-out discipline the intra ring carries.

The transfer regions are geometrically honest. They exist only at lat/long corners — the four points where a longitude channel meets a latitude channel in either the intra or extra ring. There is no re-entry transfer on the flat faces because flat faces have no latitude crossing. Doctrine is conservative: only what the geometry actually demands is stated.

=== `area_perturb`, `SeamOffset`, and the World Solver Contract

The implementation doctrine was crystallized in `docs/zoneInterconnect_geometry.adoc`. The `area_perturb` primitive is pure and local: it receives a named anchor area and a scalar `SeamOffset` magnitude, and shifts all downstream regions by that magnitude. It knows nothing about the world grid. Direction is not stored in `SeamOffset` because it is implicit in the area name — longitude-family areas produce column shifts, latitude-family areas produce row shifts.

The `WorldSeamSolver` is the only component that sees the full grid. It computes seam deltas, prefix-sums them into per-zone displacements, and calls `area_perturb` per zone with its computed offset. The pipeline is strictly one-directional and non-mutating. The algebra is formally stated with LaTeX equations in the doctrine document so that no future agent needs to re-derive it from code or renders.

=== What This Phase Means

Phase VI is the moment SignalFlow first deserves the label world physics engine rather than zone renderer. The earlier phases built increasingly honest local geometry. This phase established the laws by which local geometries compose into a global world without contradiction. That is not the same kind of advance as adding a new region family or fixing a seam glyph. It is the difference between a collection of local descriptions and a coherent physical model.

The AI failure theme runs through this phase with special sharpness. The seam perturbation algebra is simple once stated, but it requires holding a precise physical model in mind: zones as geometric objects with facing extra rings, perturbations as directed displacements from fixed anchors, the world layout as a prefix sum rather than an iterative relaxation. Every one of those commitments is exactly the kind of structural reasoning that current AI systems perform poorly under pressure. They can often reproduce the conclusion once told, but they cannot derive it from first principles when the problem is stated geometrically. This phase is therefore not only a routing architecture advance. It is further evidence for this project's second subject.

=== The Georule Algebra Arc and the Transcriber Failure Mode (v5.9.21-v5.9.22)

==== The Need for a Rule Layer

The seam perturbation doctrine established the global physics. It answered the question of how two adjacent zones must be separated at a shared seam face, and it showed that the separation could be computed in one pass as a prefix sum. What the doctrine did not provide was a language for expressing that physics at the level of human intent.

Without a rule layer, moving a zone face by a scalar delta meant writing raw geometry surgery in a snippet: fetch the named `RoutingZoneRegionFrame`, compute new `horizontalStart` and `horizontalSpan` values, apply them to each downstream zone manually, and repeat for every cascading consequence. For a single well-understood anchor that was tedious but manageable. For six anchors with asymmetric behavior and shared perimeter faces, it was not. Every non-trivial displacement required the caller to hold the full geometric consequence graph in their head, name every affected zone correctly, apply effects in the right order, and not forget any. That is not a user-facing interface. It is a trap.

The intent behind the rule layer was to close that trap. A user should be able to declare `Et += 15` — meaning: push the east chip terminal boundary outward by fifteen columns — and the engine should figure out what that implies for every other zone without the caller naming any of them. The rule bank, `RULES`, is the explicit carrier of those implications. It is a table: for each anchor zone and operation, a sequence of `(target, face, effect, factor)` entries that fully describes the cascade.

That is the crucial architectural lift. The rule bank is not a convenience wrapper. It is the symbolic encoding of the geometry's dependency graph. Once it exists, displacement is a first-class algebraic operation: inspectable, testable, and independent of any caller's knowledge of the zone family. The engine interprets the rules; the user names the anchor.

==== The Asymmetry Problem and the sfN.Z Sentinel

The rule bank is not symmetric. Different anchors produce different cascade patterns, and some of those patterns are non-obvious. The east chip terminal anchor `Et` is simple: pushing Et east means stretching the Efi inner fan east to stay abutting, stretching the Ne and Se corner zones east to cover the expanded perimeter, and translating every zone already east of Efi further east to make room. No zone needs to move west. The world origin is safe.

The west chip terminal anchor `We` is not simple. When We contracts westward — moving toward the world origin — a naive rule would translate We and all its dependents west. But the world anchor is fixed at the north-west corner. Zones cannot legally have negative column positions. The west displacement would push We and its downstream chain toward or past the origin wall.

The resolution was the `sfN.Z` sentinel: a rule entry that, when reached, applies its effect to every zone in the geometry rather than to a specific named target. Used with a factor of −1 ahead of We's own +1 translate, it acts as a global floor guard: translate the entire routing core eastward first by the displacement magnitude, then translate We back westward by the same amount. The net effect on We is zero — it holds its absolute position — while the routing core shifts east by the displacement, opening the space We needs on the west face. The north perimeter zones (Ne, Se) then have their west faces stretched to cover the new gap.

The same pattern applies to Ne. Moving the northern anchor southward means the routing core must shift south first (sfN.Z with −1), then Ne and its cluster (Nt, Nfi, Nfe) are translated back north to hold their absolute position. The long channels on all four sides (We, Wi, Ei, Ee) have their north face stretched to cover the newly opened vertical extent.

This floor guard pattern is load-bearing in the rule ordering. The sfN.Z entry must fire before the named zone's translate. If the order is reversed, the named zone translates first into a position that the subsequent Z-shift then corrupts. Several early iterations of the rule bank had this wrong. The comment in the source — "Order is load-bearing: Z fires first" — is not a style note. It is the difference between a correct displacement and a geometry that has moved a zone through its neighbor.

==== ZoneRegionCollect and the First-Class Zone Boundary

Not every displacement is symmetric in the way the Z sentinel pattern is. The east terminal rule `Et += n` does not need a floor guard because Et moves east, away from the world origin. But it does need to translate an open-ended set of zones: everything already east of the inner fan boundary Efi. Before Efi was displaced, that set was {Et, Efe, Ee}. After any geometry change, it is whatever happens to live beyond Efi. Naming those zones explicitly in the rule bank would couple the rule definition to the current zone population — a fragile dependency that breaks whenever a zone is added, removed, or renamed.

The resolution was `ZoneRegionCollect`: a descriptor type that, when used as a rule target, tells the engine to compute the affected zone set at dispatch time rather than at rule-definition time. A `ZoneRegionCollect(sfN.Efi, TopologyRegions.EAST)` entry means: at dispatch time, call `zonesCollect_get(Efi, EAST)` to produce all first-class zones whose `horizontalStart` is strictly east of Efi's `horizontalStart`, then translate all of them by the rule's applied delta.

The interaction with boundary frames required additional care. Boundary frames — module boundary overlays — are not first-class geometry zones. They are stored separately in `effectiveBoundaryFramesByName`. The `ZoneRegionCollect` dispatch needed to translate them too, but only those whose centre coordinate fell in the same directional half as the collected zones. A boundary frame whose centre is west of the anchor should not move when zones east of the anchor are translated. A predicate over the boundary frame's centre coordinate was the resolution.

Getting that predicate right was one of the longer iterations. An early version translated all boundaries regardless of position, producing incorrect module box positions for zones that had not moved. A later version omitted boundary translation entirely, leaving module boxes at their original positions after zone displacement. The correct version — translate boundaries whose centre is strictly beyond the anchor's coordinate in the collection direction — required holding the full three-part distinction (first-class zones, routing sub-regions, boundary overlays) consistently across several rounds of correction.

==== The Distinction Between First-Class Zones and Routing Sub-Regions

`zonesCollect_get` operates only on first-class zones — the entries in `geometryZonesById`. Routing sub-regions (the intra and extra latitude and longitude channel frames) are not in that collection. They are either owned inside a zone's frame or are separate routing-only frames that do not participate in the displacement algebra directly.

Several early rule applications silently included sub-regions in the zone collection because the zone id set was populated from a looser source. The geometric consequence was displacement being applied twice — once through the first-class zone, once through the sub-region — producing frame positions that bore no relation to the declared delta. The failure mode was visible only in the rendered output: frames would overlap, or a channel would appear on the wrong side of its containing zone. Each such failure required the human architect to trace which frame had moved, by how much, and why — in the rendered ASCII and in the data model simultaneously.

This is the asymmetry at the heart of the LLM failure in this arc. Reading the data model and reading the rendered output are two separate inspection acts. The LLM can be shown one or the other but cannot hold both simultaneously in a way that correctly explains the discrepancy. Shown the rendered output, it proposes a fix to the render path. Told about the data model, it proposes a fix to the rule. Only when the snippet infrastructure could produce both outputs adjacently — geometry sprint showing zone frame positions, wiring sprint showing route positions — did the chain become short enough to close quickly.

==== The Relaxation Clamping Gap

After the rule bank was nominally stable and Et displacement was producing correct geometry, the wiring render revealed a new anomaly. The nLat wires — north latitude routing channels — appeared above the Ni zone's `verticalStart` boundary after displacement. Rows 13, 14, and 15 carried wire that belonged in a zone whose origin was row 16.

The cause was in `regionFramesRelaxed_build`, a function that attempts to reduce wire pressure in the north latitude routing channel by shifting the Ni routing frame upward — toward lower row indices — in small increments. This relaxation is correct and useful for an unexplored geometry where the zone has headroom above its current routing origin. It is wrong when the zone has been displaced and the routing origin is exactly at the zone boundary. The relaxation had no knowledge of the zone geometry's `verticalStart`; it only knew the current frame position and the pressure score.

The first fix was zone-boundary clamping: record the Ni zone's `verticalStart` and Si's `verticalEnd` before the relaxation loop begins, and break the loop the moment a proposed shift would carry the routing frame outside the zone's declared extent. The relaxation loop became zone-aware.

That fix was later superseded by the current centroid-spread rule. The live solver now spreads `Ni` and `Si` as a paired move, measures realized merged-cell congestion rather than a narrow proxy score, and runs until completion or until hard bounds are reached. As a result, `SYMMETRIC` and `MINIMAL` currently behave the same for centroid spread and are parked for later design cleanup.

==== The Stale World Frame

The third failure was structurally different from the previous two. It did not involve incorrect geometry — the displacement algebra was now correct. It involved a downstream consumer that had not been updated when the geometry changed.

`boardCanvas_render` sizes its character grid from `board.worldFrame.bottomRight`. The world frame is computed once in `_boardWorldFrame_build` at board construction time and stored as a frozen attribute. After a displacement — `replace(board, geometry=afterGeo)` — the geometry reflects the new zone positions. The stored world frame does not. It retains the column and row extents of the original, undisplaced geometry.

The symptom was visible in the wiring ruler. After Et+15, the geometry sprint showed the board extending to approximately column 124. The wiring sprint ruler showed column 110 as the rightmost character and truncated everything to the right. The wiring was correct; the canvas was clipped.

The fix was to derive canvas dimensions from `board.geometry` at render time rather than from the stored world frame: three passes over region frames, boundary frames, and chip placements to collect right and bottom extents, identical to the logic in `_boardWorldFrame_build`. This bypassed the stale stored value entirely without touching the rest of the board representation.

The stale world frame was not a design error in the original code. The board was not expected to have its geometry replaced after construction. Displacement via the georule algebra was a new usage pattern. When usage patterns expand, frozen-at-build values become stale. That propagation — from "new usage possible" to "existing consumer produces wrong output" — is exactly the kind of consequence chain the LLM did not track.

==== What the Arc Reveals

Three failures, three root causes, one structural signature. In each case: a value computed from the geometry, frozen or unconstrained, consumed after the geometry had changed without the consumer being updated. The relaxation loop had no awareness of zone boundaries. The canvas had no awareness of post-build displacement. The rule bank ordering had no self-enforcing mechanism to prevent incorrect sequencing. None of these was obvious from the code in isolation. All became obvious when the truth surface — the adjacent renders produced by the snippet infrastructure — showed the output disagreeing with the geometry.

The snippet infrastructure is the methodological key to this arc. Without it, each of these failures would have required extensive verbal reasoning about which frame had moved, how far, and whether the output was measuring the right thing. With it, the human architect could point at two rendered outputs in the same terminal and ask why they disagreed. That is a falsifiable question. It has a traceable answer. It is not dependent on the LLM's ability to hold the geometry in mind.

What the arc also confirms is the exact boundary of productive LLM assistance in this domain. The model can implement a rule once the rule's semantics are fully stated in terms the model can transcribe: "add this entry to the RULES dict, factor −1, effect TRANSLATE, target sfN.Z." It cannot derive that the floor guard entry is needed, that it must precede the named zone's translate, or that the factor sign must be −1 rather than +1. Those derivations require understanding why — understanding the geometry's world model, the origin constraint, and the cascade consequence of applying effects out of order. The model has no such understanding. It has tokens and patterns. The human architect held the world model throughout. The model held the last correction.

That is the specific failure mode this arc adds to the project's record: not hallucination, not aesthetic drift, not broad architectural collapse. Correct local transcription, incorrect global consequence. The two are not distinguishable from the code surface alone. They only separate under the falsification pressure of a deterministic truth surface.

=== The Scorer Truth Arc and the User-Driven Falsification (v5.9.33-v5.9.34)

By v5.9.33 the centroid relaxation had been formally correct for some time. The loop ran. It paired Ni with Si as a coupled north/south spread. It clamped against the geometry zone boundaries the previous arc had taught it to respect. It terminated either at a zero score or when no further legal shift was available. Every test that asserted its mechanics passed. The function `regionFramesRelaxed_build` was, by every internal measurement the project applied to it, working.

It was not working. It was optimizing the wrong objective, and the only way that fact became visible was a second-order disagreement between two truth surfaces that had been quietly drifting apart for several versions.

==== Status Quo: Visible Wires, Hidden Objective

The presenting symptom was a routing collision in `examples/simple-circuit/back-and-forth.yaml` zone `(1,3)`. Three back-edge return wires — `ggc1ret`, `ggc2ret`, and `ggc3ret` — converge through the south latitude routing band on their way back from the great-grandchild chips to the grandchild parents. At the realization the engine produced, two of those wires shared cell `(81, 30)` co-linearly: `ggc2ret` arriving on the `sLat[1]` row westbound, and `ggc3ret` arriving horizontally on the chip-terminal entry row of `gc1`'s east face — also row 30, by chip placement. Two wires, one row, same cells. The kind of collision the relaxation should have spread away from.

The architect's first reading was that this was an architectural blocker: the `sLat[1]` row coincided with a chip terminal entry row, and no amount of paired Ni/Si spread could relieve a collision that lived on a row pinned by chip placement. That reading was reported to the user with confidence. The user disagreed. The exchange that followed is one of the cleanest examples in the project's history of an LLM holding a plausible but unverified explanation while the human architect held the actual question.

==== The User Disagrees with the Architect

The user's challenge was direct: "I disagree. Wouldn't pushing the Si channel one more row south provide a clean separation between ggc2ret and ggc3ret?" That is a falsifiable question. It can be answered by setting Si.verticalStart one row further south and counting whether the collision persists.

The answer required a probe. The probe loaded zone `(1,3)`, walked the relaxation step function K=7 through K=10, and at each step computed both the score the relaxation actually used and a per-cell breakdown showing which signal IDs collided where. The first run produced a result that should have been impossible: every K from 8 through 10 reported the same total collision count of 22. The relaxation had no reason to prefer K=8 over K=9, and the tie-break rule had silently picked the first encountered minimum.

That tie should not have existed. K=8 placed a co-linear collision on `sLat[1]` at row 30 — the chip-terminal row, structurally pinned. K=9 moved that collision to row 31 — a free interior row that downstream lane reassignment could in principle relieve. The two configurations were not equivalent under any architectural reading the project would endorse. Yet the scorer found them indistinguishable. Something in the score itself was lying.

==== The Hidden Regex Mismatch

The mistake was three lines of code and four years of pattern-matching habit. `_realizedCollisionCount_calculate` distinguished real collisions from legitimate same-signal multi-drop fanout by extracting a signal ID from each route's path text and counting only cells where two distinct signal IDs claimed the cell. The extraction used the regex `\[id=([^\]]+)\]`. That regex assumed the route's path text contained an annotation of the form `App.ts.main().s1 [id=s1]::wf[0]::...`.

The route texts the scorer actually received did not contain that annotation. They were produced by `_algebraicPathText_build`, which serializes a structured `AlgebraicPath` plus a lane map into a `source::lanes::sink` string with no `[id=]` brackets. The two formats coexisted in the codebase: `solvedWire.wireText_get()` produces the annotated form (it is built explicitly in `_endpointDisplayText_build`), and `_algebraicPathText_build` produces the bare form. The collision detector in `materialized_runtime` consumed the annotated form. The collision scorer in `realizer` consumed the bare form. The scorer's regex had been written for the wrong consumer.

The consequence was specific and silent. The regex never matched. The fallback assigned `signalId = algebraicPathText` — the entire path text, unique per route. Every shared cell registered as a collision between two distinct signals, including legitimate same-signal multi-drop fanout where the same back-edge return signal genuinely visits two parent sinks. The score landscape was over-counted by a fixed amount that did not change with K, so the topology of the landscape was preserved well enough that the relaxation appeared to descend, but the absolute scores were lifted into a regime where pinned-row collisions and free-row collisions weighed the same.

The fix was to extract the signal ID from the sink token of the path text — the trailing segment after the final `.`, which both serialization formats agree on because both end with `.signalName`. A four-line change. With the corrected extract, the K=7 score dropped from 40 to 28, K=8 from 38 to 22, and K=9 to 22 — exposing the genuine tie that the over-counting had previously hidden inside the inflation.

==== Terminal-Row Weighting and the Pinned-Cell Insight

The corrected scorer produced honest counts but still rated K=8 and K=9 as equivalent. That equivalence was itself an architectural error. A collision on a chip-terminal row cannot be relieved by paired Ni/Si spread; the row index is pinned by chip placement, and any wire entering or exiting through that face must touch it. A collision on a free interior row can in principle be relieved by lane reassignment downstream. The two are not equally costly. The scorer was treating them as equally costly because it had no concept of which rows were pinned.

The information was already in the route inputs. Each `RealizerRouteInput` carries `sourceAttachPoint` and `destinationAttachPoint` as world coordinates: those rows are exactly the chip-terminal entry rows the wires must touch. Collecting them into a `terminalRows` set during the same loop that builds `cellSignals`, and then assigning weight 2 to collisions whose row appears in that set and weight 1 elsewhere, gives the relaxation a faithful objective without inventing any new geometric machinery. The penalty K=8 became 38, K=9 became 34, K=10 became 34. K=9 strictly beat K=8 by four units, all attributable to two pinned-row collisions at row 30 that K=9 had pushed off the terminal row to row 31.

The relaxation now picks K=9 for `back-and-forth.yaml` zone `(1,3)`. The `(81, 30)` co-linear collision the user originally pointed at is gone. One residual collision remains at `(82, 30)`: the corner cell where `ggc2ret`'s eLong-to-sLat bend coincides with `ggc3ret`'s chip-terminal entry. Both wires must pass through that cell regardless of Ni/Si shift. That one is genuinely architectural — it requires an eLong lane reassignment, not a paired spread. The scorer correctly identifies it as residual; the relaxation correctly does not waste further iterations attempting to remove it.

==== The Display That Lied: Initial vs Relaxed Geometry

While the scorer was being corrected, a second truth-surface drift came to light. The `BoardMaterializedSolution.geometry_sprint` method was rendering the geometry from `board.geometry.regionFramesByName` — the *initial* frame map — even though the wires being rendered had been realized against the *relaxed* frame map produced by `regionFramesRelaxed_build`. The two maps could and routinely did differ by several rows, and during this exact debugging session they differed by nine rows for both Ni and Si. The user was seeing wires drawn at one set of row positions inside region boundaries drawn at a different set. Every visual diagnosis built from that sprint was anchored to a frame the realizer had not used.

The scope of the drift was its most dangerous property. The relaxation had been working through several versions while the geometry sprint had been showing the unrelaxed frames. None of the project's tests caught it because the tests asserted on the wire geometry text, not on the legend frame extents. The user noticed it only because the relaxation step under investigation moved Ni from row 18 to row 9 and Si from row 22 to row 31 — a nine-row shift large enough to make the legend visibly disagree with the wire positions.

The fix needed to preserve the relaxed frames on the materialized solution and render them through a shadow Board variant whose `BoardGeometry` carried the relaxed frames. `realizationPlan.regionFramesByName` was retained on `BoardMaterializedSolution` as `_regionFramesByName`, exposed via two new public surfaces — `relaxedRegionFrames_get()` returning the dict and `geometryRelaxed_sprint(legend_show=True)` returning the rendered text. The existing `geometry_sprint()` was rewired to render through `_relaxedShadowBoard_build()`, which constructs a new `BoardGeometry` via the `regionFramesById=` constructor using a name-to-regionId mapping, and substitutes that into a `replace(self.board, geometry=...)` shadow. The original board is not mutated.

The `dir()` listing on `BoardMaterializedSolution` gained two new entries — `geometryRelaxed_text` and `relaxedRegionFrames_get` — placed in the correct ASCII sort position. The test asserting the dir() listing failed when the entries were placed naively; the failure was the test catching that ASCII order treats `R` (82) as less than `_` (95), so `relaxedRegionFrames_get` sorts after `occupancy_text` and before `summary_text` rather than alphabetically among the `r`-prefixed methods. A small correctness ratchet of the kind the project has learned to value.

==== The Detector Key Alignment

A third defect came out of the same investigation. `_collisionReport_build` in `materialized_runtime` runs a separate per-cell collision detector intended for human-facing diagnostics. It collects per-cell wire claims into `cellClaims` and per-cell direction sets into `cellDirectionsByWire`, keyed by `wireText`, and uses the direction sets to filter perpendicular crossings out of the collision report (a perpendicular crossing in a transition region is expected geometry, not a collision). The two collectors had silently been keyed against different `wireText` values for the same wire, with the result that the perpendicular-crossing filter was always falling back to an empty set lookup and silently classifying every shared cell as perpendicular.

The mismatch came from how the two collectors were populated. `cellClaims` was built in a loop that read `materializedWire.solvedWire.wireText_get()`. `cellDirectionsByWire` had been built in a separate loop that walked the realized routes by index and constructed a synthetic key from the source and destination chip references. Those two strings were structurally different — `wireText_get()` includes the `[id=]` annotations and uses the full `source:destination` form, while the synthetic key omitted both. Subset comparison on empty sets always succeeded, so every collision was suppressed as perpendicular, and the human-facing report showed zero collisions while the underlying realization carried real ones.

The fix paired the two iterators: `for materializedWire, realizedRoute in zip(self._materializedWires, self._realizedRouteSet.realizedRoutes)`, with `wireText` derived once from the materialized wire and used as the key for both collectors. With the alignment in place, roughly seventy-six previously hidden cell-claim entries surfaced. Most were same-signal multi-drop fanout that the regex-based filter (now applied via `_signalId_extract` against the corrected key) correctly excluded. Two were the genuine `(81, 30)` and `(82, 30)` co-linear collisions the user had originally been asking about.

==== What This Arc Reveals

The arc restates the project's central truth in a sharper form. Three independent defects were live in the codebase simultaneously, all of them in working code, none of them visible from any single artifact the project routinely inspected. The relaxation was descending against an objective inflated by false-positive same-signal fanout. The geometry sprint was rendering against a frame map the realizer had not used. The collision detector was filtering against a key it could never match. Every one of these passed every test the project had. Every one of these was caught only when two truth surfaces — the score landscape and the visual render — disagreed in a way that could not be reconciled by re-reading either one in isolation.

The user-driven falsification matters as a methodological data point. The architect's initial reading — "this is architectural, no Ni/Si shift can fix it" — was plausible, internally consistent, and wrong. The user's counter-reading — "would one more row south fix it?" — was a direct empirical question that the existing code could be asked. Asking it produced a probe. The probe produced numbers. The numbers exposed the regex mismatch. The mismatch, once fixed, exposed the missing weight. The weight, once added, validated the user's original intuition. None of that chain was reachable from the architect's first answer. The user's willingness to disagree with a confidently-stated but unverified claim was the load-bearing methodological act.

This is the form the human-LLM dynamic continues to take in this project. The model can implement scoring weights once the weight semantics are stated. It can fix a regex once the regex's intended target is stated. It can build a shadow Board once the shadow's purpose is stated. It cannot derive any of those needs from the existing code. It will, by default, defend its first reading rather than re-examine its premises, because re-examination requires a kind of empirical humility that the model does not have access to without external pressure. The pressure has to come from the architect, and it has to come in the form of falsifiable questions rather than confidence-graded assertions. The user supplied that pressure here. The model supplied the implementation. The arc closed in one session. The lesson is not new but the proof is fresh.

The residual `(82, 30)` collision is the cleanest possible signature of the new equilibrium. It is genuinely architectural — both wires must touch that cell, no Ni/Si shift can change it, and the relaxation correctly does not waste iterations on it. The scorer reports it as residual and stops. The user can see it in the render. The project knows what it is and what would fix it (eLong lane reassignment in the east transition band). It is the kind of remaining problem the next phase can pick up cleanly because the layer beneath it is no longer lying.

== Phase VII: World Assembly and the Prefix-Sum Epoch (v6.0.0 - present)
_Period: April 2026_

=== The Milestone That Permits the Next Problem

Phase VI ended with a property that had not been true at any prior point in the project: every zone in the active fixture materialized independently with correct wiring. Zone `(1,1)`, zone `(1,2)`, and zone `(1,3)` of `examples/simple-circuit/back-and-forth.yaml` each produced a collision-free realized route set. The relaxation chose the geometrically honest spread. The collision detector reported what was actually there. The renders showed what the realizer had used.

That collective correctness is not a cosmetic threshold. It is the only condition under which multi-zone composition is a well-defined problem. Composing zones whose individual materialization is wrong produces a world that inherits every zone-local error as a world-level fact, and then adds cross-zone errors on top. Composition of individually correct zones is a different problem: it is purely geometric and has an exact algebraic answer. The version bump from 5.9.x to 6.0.0 marks that transition. The 5.x series was the work of making per-zone materialization honest. The 6.x series is the work of making the world assembly honest.

=== The Physical Model Is Already Written

The surprising thing about the world assembly problem — surprising only in retrospect — is that the algebra to solve it had already been written. `docs/zoneInterconnect_geometry.adoc` defined `ZoneGeometryMutation`, `ZoneAdjacencyConstraint`, and the outline of `WorldGeometryResolver` before the per-zone wiring was even stable. The seam perturbation doctrine had established directed displacement. The `area_perturb` primitive and the georule algebra had demonstrated that named-anchor displacement was a typed, testable operation. The prefix-sum propagation formula was there in the doctrine document as an explicit LaTeX expression.

This is unusual in the project's history. Most phases discovered their algebra under pressure: the seam model was wrong before the overlap model was right, the chip-local manifold was inadequate before the board-era substrate was coherent, the relaxation was over-counting before the scorer was corrected. Phase VII opens instead with a pre-existing algebra and the question of whether the implementation can be made to match it faithfully. That is a different kind of engineering work. It is closer to a proof exercise than a discovery.

=== What World Assembly Means in the Existing Algebra

For a chain of zones stem:[Z_{1,1} \to Z_{1,2} \to Z_{1,3}] arranged west-to-east, world assembly requires computing and applying the horizontal budget at each seam. The budget at seam stem:[A \to B] is:

[stem]
++++
H(A, B) = \Delta T(A, B) + P^{\min}(A, B)
++++

where stem:[\Delta T(A,B) = \max(T^{E}_{A}, T^{W}_{B}) - T^{E}_{A}] is the terminal-face harmonization delta and stem:[P^{\min}(A,B) = X^{E}_{A} + X^{W}_{B}] is the minimum extra-ring clearance budget for the facing extra-ring spans of the two zones.

Applying stem:[H(A,B)] to zone stem:[A]'s eastern geometry is precisely the existing `Et += n` georule: translate stem:[\mathcal{C}(\text{Efi}, \text{EAST})] by stem:[+H], stretch `Efi.east`, stretch `Ne.east`, stretch `Se.east`. No new primitive is needed. The georule algebra already knows how to do this.

The cascade property — that stem:[H(A,B)] depends on how wide stem:[B]'s west face is after stem:[H(B,C)] has already been absorbed — translates into a processing order constraint: traverse the zone tree leaf-first, collect all budgets, then apply prefix sums from the anchor outward. The world displacement formula

[stem]
++++
\Delta x(i,j) = \sum_{k=1}^{j-1} H_{i,k}
++++

is the direct algebraic statement of that order. `WorldGeometryResolver` is the object responsible for managing that traversal and accumulation. Its contract is already stated in the doctrine document. The implementation is the missing part.

The vertical axis follows the same pattern. stem:[V(A,B)] at a north/south seam uses the facing stem:[T^{N}]/stem:[T^{S}] terminal extents and the north/south extra-ring spans stem:[X^{N}_{A}], stem:[X^{S}_{B}]. The Ne and We georules already express how north-face perturbation cascades through the zone collection. Axis resolution is handled by the anchor-token axis rule: N/S prefixes produce vertical displacement, E/W prefixes produce horizontal displacement. Both axes share the same primitive algebra.

=== Why the Cascade Must Be Bottom-Up

The order of budget computation is not a style preference. If zone stem:[B]'s We (west extra ring) width depends on how much stem:[B]'s own inner geometry has been widened to accommodate zone stem:[C], then the stem:[H(A,B)] budget cannot be computed until stem:[H(B,C)] is settled. That dependency chain, in a tree of depth stem:[d], forces a post-order traversal: leaves first, root last. Any attempt to process the tree in document order — zone stem:[(1,1)] first — will compute stem:[H(1,1 \to 1,2)] against a We(1,2) that has not yet been widened by stem:[H(1,2 \to 1,3)]. The resulting world is geometrically inconsistent: zone (1,2) fits against zone (1,1) in column space, but zone (1,3) overlaps zone (1,2)'s east extra ring because zone (1,2) was never told to widen.

This is the exact algorithmic property the user first named in the discussion that opened this phase: "perturbations cascade." The cascade direction is bottom-up for collection, top-down for application. `WorldGeometryResolver` must implement both passes. The budget collection pass is a recursive descent. The displacement application pass is a prefix-sum accumulation that respects the fixed north-west anchor invariant stem:[\Delta x \ge 0, \Delta y \ge 0].

=== What Changes and What Stays Fixed

The fixed north-west anchor at stem:[(1,1)] is not a convention. It is the physical invariant that makes prefix-sum propagation valid. If the anchor could move, the prefix-sum formula would have no fixed basis. The anchor is therefore treated as a geometric axiom: zone stem:[(1,1)]'s geometry is placed first and is not displaced by any budget. All eastern and southern zones accumulate displacement relative to stem:[(1,1)]'s realized frame.

What changes: every zone east of column 1 and every zone south of row 1 may receive a non-zero stem:[(\Delta x, \Delta y)] world offset. The zone's local geometry is unchanged — its region spans, lane counts, and solver capacity are exactly what per-zone materialization produced. Only its world-coordinate anchor shifts. The realized route cells shift with it, because route cells are expressed in world coordinates derived from the board geometry at solve time and the board geometry is what carries the world offset.

What does not change: the symbolic solve and the realized route structure. A solved wire `nLat[2]@(source)::eLong[4]@(sink)` encodes local lane identity. World assembly translates the world coordinates of that wire, not the lane identity. The wire does not change families. The board does not re-solve. The displacement is purely geometric, applied after materialization, before the final world canvas is composed.

=== The Remaining Open Question

The algebra assumes the budget stem:[H(A,B)] is determined entirely by zone geometry: extra ring spans, terminal face extents. That assumption holds when zone geometry is determined a priori from `BoardGeometrySpec` and does not depend on the solved wire set. In the current system that assumption is valid: zone geometry is built from circuit and chip geometry, then the solver runs within that geometry, and the geometry does not change after the solve. Demand-driven feedback — where wire pressure determines extra ring sizing, which changes budgets, which changes world layout, which changes what wires must do — is a future concern identified in the doctrine document. It is not in scope for v6.x. The prefix-sum model is correct for the current system. If demand-driven feedback is eventually added, the world resolver becomes iterative rather than one-pass, and the doctrine document already says so explicitly.

=== What v6.0.0 Represents

Version numbers in this project have tracked architectural phase transitions rather than feature counts. The 5.x series carried the board-era re-architecture and the zone-overlap physics from first principles through proven per-zone materialization. The 6.x series carries world assembly from the algebra in the doctrine document through the first correct multi-zone composed render.

That composed render — `back-and-forth.yaml` drawn as one coherent world with zone geometries harmonized at every seam — is the target artifact of Phase VII. It is not the last artifact the project needs. The demand-driven feedback loop, the symbolic topology layer, the child-to-self routing problem, and the interactive board-as-REPL surface are all further phases. But the composed render is the first artifact in which the world, and not just its parts, is geometrically honest.

=== What v6.0.2 Corrects

The first world-inspect falsification showed that the vertical seam model could not be derived from north-stack accumulation alone. The user pointed at zones `(1,2)` and `(1,3)`: zone `(1,3)` placed `Ne` on rows `17..20`, while zone `(1,2)` had available north-face row space at `15..18`. The missing two rows were not arbitrary. They were `Nt` and `Nfi`, which means the relaxation budget at a north face must include `Ne + Nt + Nfi`, not `Ne` alone.

That correction removed the ghost of the earlier oversized `Ne` span. `Ne` and `Se` keep their four-lane spans where appropriate, while `Ei` and `Wi` retain the already-correct horizontal stretching behavior. More importantly, the vertical seam law changed from "accumulate north stack height" to "align shared chip rows." On the current `world_zone_inspect.py` truth surface, the `1,2` / `1,3` seam aligns `module/grandchild.ts` on rows `25..48` on both sides. Zone `(1,2)` exposes `Et` on rows `25..48`; zone `(1,3)` exposes `Wt` on rows `25..48`.

This became the production extraction target. The snippet-proven world harmonization had to become `WorldGeometryResolver` without introducing a seam-chip override, transplanting `Wt` positions, or reopening the old north-stack explanation as the active problem.

=== What v6.0.3 Adds

Version 6.0.3 moves the next layer of the world-inspect proof into board-owned production code. `WorldGeometryResolver` remains responsible for ordered-chain harmonization and `wOffset` calculation. `BoardWorldMaterializedSolution` now owns the materialized aggregate: it builds each harmonized board, preserves re-originated offsets, and exposes the geometry and wiring text surfaces that `world_zone_inspect.py` prints.

That change matters because the snippet no longer owns materialization or world canvas algebra. It remains a targeted truth surface for YAML/context setup and visual inspection, while the reusable board package owns the resolver, materialized boards, and render composition. The new regression check verifies the `1,2` / `1,3` geometry and wiring surfaces, including the `grandchild.ts` seam evidence.

=== What v6.0.4 Adds

Version 6.0.4 promotes that world aggregate to the main new-engine renderer. `signalflow file.yaml` now renders the full harmonized world circuit by default. `--zone`, `--zones`, `--geometry`, and `--wiring` expose the snippet-proven inspection controls at the CLI while still harmonizing against the full active zone chain.

=== What v6.0.5 Adds

Version 6.0.5 makes omitted-return semantics literal. A YAML port declaration that omits `return` is now forward-only: chip drawing compacts to the declared signal rows, the kernel and seam solvers attach to semantic terminal offsets, and no blank reverse route is emitted. A present empty return label (`return: ""`) is invalid. The new `neural-network.yaml` fixture exercises a 4-input, 3-hidden, 2-output forward-only fan-out circuit.

=== What v6.0.6 Adds

Version 6.0.6 documents and tests forward-wiring DAG behavior. The neural-network examples now distinguish compact fan-in display aliases from explicit per-edge destination input labels, and the world harmonizer keeps hidden/output layer chip projections coherent after seam-side module translation. The layer examples also make the current structural requirement explicit: module boundaries are real geometry today, while synthetic call-stack-depth layer envelopes remain future design work.

=== What v6.0.7 Adds

Default behavior (`--modulepolicy cross`) renders one boundary box per module spanning all depth layers. `column` renders one box per `(module, depth)` pair. `none` suppresses all boundary boxes. When `--modulepolicy` is given, `--depthbox` is silently ignored.

=== What v6.0.9 Adds

Version 6.0.9 adds the `--routing [manhattan|none]` CLI flag. `manhattan` (default) is the existing outward centroid relaxation. `none` skips centroid relaxation entirely — all other routing algebra (solver, algebraic paths, lane assignment, wire glyphs) is unchanged. The policy is threaded through `WorldRenderOptions` → `BoardMaterializePolicy.skipRelaxation` → `regionFramesRelaxed_build`. Useful as a diagnostic to observe the collision pressure that relaxation resolves.

=== What v6.0.10 Adds

Version 6.0.10 adds `--routing paris`, a band-swapped centroid relaxation policy. Paris swaps the starting positions of `Ni` and `Si` before relaxation: `Ni` begins at `Si`'s centroid slot and relaxes south toward `Sfi`; `Si` begins at `Ni`'s centroid slot and relaxes north toward `Nfi`. Forward wires attached to `Ni` therefore route through the south side of the board. This produces compact layouts without large northward arcs for forward-only topologies. For topologies with topologically required signal crossings the crossing structure is preserved but relocated to the south routing zone. Paris uses the same collision-score gating as manhattan and is guaranteed collision-free.

=== What v6.0.11 Adds

Version 6.0.11 is a bug-fix release. Two defects found by code review are corrected. First, `--depthbox` was dead when `--modulepolicy` was also supplied: the `rawModulePolicy is not None` branch hardcoded `depthBox = False`, silently discarding the flag. Fixed to `depthBox = arguments.depthbox` so `--modulepolicy none --depthbox` works as documented. Second, `wiring_sprint` used `indexes[0]` as the re-origination anchor, which caused a wrong-offset world canvas when the first requested index was not materialized. Fixed to `activeIndexes[0]`.

=== What v6.0.12 Adds

Version 6.0.12 is a bug-fix release with two corrections. First, module boundary box padding was hardcoded to 1 cell in `_moduleScopesCross_compute` and `_moduleScopesColumn_compute`; the config-level `moduleBoxPadding` (default 2, overridable via `world.geometry.moduleBoxPadding` in YAML) is now wired through `boardGeometryConfig` and used by both functions. Second, `_routeOverlayGlyph_build` in `render.py` treated any non-space, non-double-wire glyph as opaque: when a routing wire landed on a cell already occupied by a single-line chip stub (`─` or `│`), the routing wire was silently dropped, causing it to appear one cell displaced (e.g. `─║──` instead of `╫──`). Fixed so `─` pierced by a vertical track produces `╫` and `│` pierced by a horizontal track produces `╪`.

=== What v6.0.13 Adds

Version 6.0.13 fixes the wire-crossing algebra at module boundary box walls. Three defects cooperated to produce `─║──` instead of `╫──` where an east-going route exits a module box. First, `_routeOverlayGlyph_build` treated any non-space, non-double-wire glyph as opaque; a single-line chip stub `─` at a cell blocked the routing wire from being applied. Fixed so `─` pierced by a vertical track produces `╫` and `│` pierced by a horizontal track produces `╪`. Second, `_modulePaddingCell_check` used inclusive bounds for the module frame, classifying the module box wall cells themselves as padding and suppressing routing tracks there. Fixed to strict interior bounds so wall cells are never classified as padding. Third, `_worldBlit_apply` dropped a `╫` glyph (crossing already composed by another zone's render) when the world canvas already held `║` at the same cell, because both are in `_WIRE_V` and the same-axis fallback returned the existing glyph. Fixed so incoming crossing glyphs (`╫`, `╪`) take priority over plain wire glyphs on the same axis.

=== What v6.0.15 Adds

Version 6.0.15 corrects the visual layering when `--modulepolicy column --depthbox` are combined. Previously both module boxes and depth boxes computed their frames from the same base `moduleBoxPadding`, so they fought for the same pixel boundary — the module top would overwrite the depth label or vice versa depending on blit order. The fix adds `extra_pad=2` to module scope builders only when `depthBox` is active (`module_extra_pad=2 if depthBox else 0` at both `geometry_sprint` and `wiring_sprint` call sites). Module boxes are now the outer border (double-line `╔═╗`) and depth boxes are the inner border (single-line `┌─┐`), correctly nested with visible space between them. Module-only and depth-box-only renders are unaffected.

=== What v6.0.14 Adds

Version 6.0.14 adds YAML-driven depth-layer labels for the `--depthbox` rendering mode. A new top-level `depth_labels` list in any circuit YAML supplies user-defined names for each calling-stack depth; `depth_labels[0]` is the root depth. Labels are applied only when `--depthbox` is explicitly passed on the CLI; presence of `depth_labels` in the YAML does not implicitly enable depth-box rendering.

Two fundamental bugs blocked the feature from working correctly and were fixed together. First, depth-scope frames in the board geometry are computed from visible chip draw bounds (which include port-stub lines extending across the full zone width), so both depth scopes within any overlap zone ended up with identical bounding frames. A new `_depthScopesBody_compute` function re-derives depth frames from chip body bounds (`worldFrame_get()`) instead, yielding distinct non-overlapping boxes for each depth. Second, per-zone depth-box characters were blitted to the world canvas with a `glyph != " "` guard that skipped the spaces inside label strings, leaving routing-wire artifacts visible behind the label text. Fixed by moving depth-box drawing to a world-level post-blit pass (`_worldDepthBoxes_blit`) that writes directly onto the world grid after all zone blits are complete, force-overwriting routing glyphs without skipping spaces.

The example YAML `examples/neural-network-4-3-2.yaml` gains a `depth_labels` block assigning "Network", "Input Layer", "Hidden Layer", and "Output Layer" to the four calling-stack depths of that circuit.

== Closing Note

SignalFlow's history is unusual because the project itself became the experiment. A deterministic geometry engine built to reveal code structure ended up revealing something else with equal force: how weak current AI systems remain at sustained architectural reasoning when correctness depends on exact ownership, exact geometry, and exact consequence.

That is not a side note. It is now one of the main reasons this project matters.

The arc from Phase I to Phase VI traces a particular kind of intellectual maturation. The project began with the question of whether geometry could carry structural truth. It learned, painfully and repeatedly, that geometry can only carry structural truth if the geometry itself is owned, derived from first principles, and governed by a coherent internal physics. Every phase in which that discipline was relaxed produced exactly the kind of drift and regression that made AI assistance most dangerous and most tempting at the same time. Every phase in which it was tightened produced the only kind of progress that held: provable, inspectable, and resistant to the pattern-matching failures that had accumulated in the layers below.

The project now has a world physics. It is not a complete one. The symbolic algebra for routes that cross ring boundaries is unwritten. The demand-driven feedback loop between wire pressure and extra ring expansion is unimplemented. The child-to-self routing problem — a route that leaves a zone's intra substrate, travels the perimeter, and returns specifically to the originating chip rather than to any chip in the zone — remains the hardest unresolved question in the system. But the foundation is now honest. The geometry descends from declared truth rather than ascending from accumulated heuristics. That inversion is what Phase V and Phase VI were actually about, and it is what makes the next phases tractable rather than merely optimistic.

The v5.9.33 to v5.9.34 scorer-truth arc is also a reminder that "honest foundation" is a property the project has to keep paying for. Three independent defects had been live in working code, surviving every test and every routine inspection, until two truth surfaces — the score landscape and the visual render — were forced to disagree by a user-driven empirical question. The architect's first reading was wrong. The user's challenge was right. The model held the implementation, but the falsifying question came from outside the model. That is the rhythm the project has settled into, and it is the rhythm the remaining open problems will continue to require.

The v6.0.0 version bump marks a different kind of transition. For the first time, the advance that opens a new phase is not the correction of a defect in the existing layer. It is the completion of one problem class — per-zone materialization — and the promotion of the next problem class — world assembly — to active status. The algebra for world assembly was written before the per-zone problem was clean enough to permit it. That sequence — doctrine before implementation, algebra before code — is not accidental. It is the methodological lesson the project has been arriving at for six phases. The world can only be assembled correctly when the parts are individually honest, and the assembly algorithm can only be stated correctly when the local mutation language already exists to express it. Both conditions now hold. The 6.x series begins from a position the 5.x series had to earn.
