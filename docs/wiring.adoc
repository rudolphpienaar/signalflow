= SignalFlow Wiring Specification: Graph Theory and Topology
Rudolph Pienaar <rudolph.pienaar@childrens.harvard.edu>
v1.5, March 2026

This document formalizes the topological rules and routing heuristics used by the SignalFlow engine to render recursive call trees as 2D ASCII schematics. The paradigm prioritizes **Topological Faithfulness** over visual simplicity, ensuring that every functional unit is unique and every data-path is explicitly routed.

== The Graph Model

SignalFlow has transitioned from a Tree-based renderer to a **Signal Flow Graph (SFG)** model.

=== Unique Function Chips
Every functional unit (defined by its `module:func` identifier) appears **exactly once** in the diagram. If a function is called multiple times (e.g., a shared `Logger` or `Hub`), it is rendered as a single chip with multiple incoming and outgoing ports.

=== Registry-Based Discovery
The engine uses a global registry to deduplicate functional units during the YAML parsing phase. This allows for "Incremental Discovery," where different branches of the call tree contribute to the master definition of a shared chip.

=== Port Pool Management
Shared chips (Hubs) maintain a pool of `unbound_inputs` and `unbound_outputs`. When a parent calls a Hub without explicitly defining labels, the engine sequentially binds the connection to the next available port in the Hub's pool. This ensures that even anonymous callers are correctly labeled on the Hub's walls.

== Coordinate System

* **+x (Columns):** Increases from Left to Right.
* **+y (Rows):** Increases from Top to Bottom.

== Channel Staggering Heuristics

=== Unified Staggering Rule
The stagger index for any connection between `parent` and `child` is defined by the maximum of its port indices:
`stagger_idx = max(parent_output_index, child_input_index)`

=== Vertical Affinity (The Manifold Rule)
To prevent horizontal wires from piercing vertical tracks in a manifold, the vertical track closest to the destination wall (Rightmost) corresponds to the port encountered **first** by the vertical flow.

* **Descending Flow:** Top-most port is Rightmost.
* **Ascending Flow:** Bottom-most port is Rightmost.

=== The Double Helix Rule (Argument vs. Return)
To prevent forward and return wires from crossing each other, side-assignment flips based on vertical direction:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Vertical Direction | Relative Topology
| **Descending** (`src_y < dst_y`) | Return is to the **LEFT** of Argument (X vs. X+1).
| **Ascending** (`src_y > dst_y`) | Return is to the **RIGHT** of Argument (X vs. X-1).
|===

== The Geometric Terminator Algebra

The core rendering engine has evolved from "character-stamping" to a **Vector-Aware Intent Engine**. This model treats every wire placement as a mathematical addition of directional vectors rather than a simple overwrite.

=== The State-Intent Duality
Schematic synthesis is governed by the interaction between two layers:

1. **Topological State:** The 16 potential connectivity states of a canvas cell (documented as the "16-Character Set").
2. **Drawing Intent:** The atomic directional vectors (N, S, E, W) being applied by a drawing primitive (the "Intent").

=== The 16-Character Vocabulary
To support rigorous algebra, the engine uses a 16-character set that includes **Terminator Stubs**. These stubs represent wires that start or end within a cell, allowing the system to distinguish between a junction and a crossing.

[cols="1,1,2"]
|===
| Character | Bitmask | Description
| `' '` | 0 | Empty
| `╵` | 1 | **N-Stub**: Enters from South, terminates North.
| `╷` | 2 | **S-Stub**: Enters from North, terminates South.
| `│` | 3 | **Pass-V**: Vertical pass-through (N | S).
| `╶` | 4 | **E-Stub**: Enters from West, terminates East.
| `└` | 5 | **NE Elbow**: Corner turning North and East.
| `┌` | 6 | **SE Elbow**: Corner turning South and East.
| `├` | 7 | **NSE Junction**: Vertical line branching East.
| `╴` | 8 | **W-Stub**: Enters from East, terminates West.
| `┘` | 9 | **NW Elbow**: Corner turning North and West.
| `┐` | 10 | **SW Elbow**: Corner turning South and West.
| `┤` | 11 | **NSW Junction**: Vertical line branching West.
| `─` | 12 | **Pass-H**: Horizontal pass-through (E | W).
| `┴` | 13 | **NEW Junction**: Horizontal line branching North.
| `┬` | 14 | **SEW Junction**: Horizontal line branching South.
| `┼` | 15 | **NSEW Crossing**: Independent 4-way intersection.
|===

=== The Algebraic Truth Table
The `LayoutJoiner` module enforces an exhaustive 16x16 algebraic matrix. The result of merging any **Existing Character** with a new **Drawing Intent** is deterministic:

`ResultGlyph = ExistingState + IntentVector`

[cols="1,4*^1"]
|===
| Existing \ Intent | **N (↑)** | **S (↓)** | **E (→)** | **W (←)**
| **─ (Pass-H)** | `┴` (Branch Up) | `┬` (Branch Down) | `─` (No Change) | `─` (No Change)
| **│ (Pass-V)** | `│` (No Change) | `│` (No Change) | `├` (Branch Right) | `┤` (Branch Left)
| **┌ (SE Elbow)** | `├` (Branch Right) | `┌` (Leg Exists) | `┌` (Leg Exists) | `┬` (Branch Down)
| **┼ (Crossing)** | `┼` | `┼` | `┼` | `┼`
|===

=== Visual Promotion Layer
To maintain a high-fidelity "Physical Circuit" aesthetic, intermediate mathematical stubs (`╵`, `╷`, `╶`, `╴`) are used for internal calculations but are **Promoted** to full-line characters (`│`, `─`) in the final output string. This preserves topological memory while ensuring visual continuity.

== Internal Wiring Manifold (The Turtle Matrix)

Inside a functional chip, arbitrary point-to-point routing is supported via the
`internal_wiring` directive. Current syntax supports additive orientation tokens
(`EW`, `WE`, `NS`, `SN`), route-class modifiers (`:data`, `:thread`), semantic
modifiers (`:pure`, `:compute`), and presentation modifiers such as
`:color(name)`, with the label order always read as source→destination.
Same-wall adjacent handoffs are rendered as local wall continuities; true
manifold paths retain wall-specific endpoint identity even when the display
label is reused on both walls. `:data` and `:thread` do not change routing
geometry; they only change semantic and presentational treatment, with
`:thread` defaulting to compute when neither `:pure` nor `:compute` is given.
`:color(none)` forces one route to remain monochrome. A right-wall `ret -> sig`
handoff that overlaps existing east-side manifold bundles uses the renderer's
*east-edge manifold
reuse pattern*: the handoff is realized as a short local bridge that reuses the
existing fan-out and fan-in rows instead of drawing a detached east-edge loop.

=== Context-Aware Merging (The "Inside/Outside" Rule)
To prevent the topological algebra from corrupting module boxes and labels, the engine implements **Mode-Specific Merging**:

* **Inside the Chip (`mode_merge=True`):** The `set()` primitive uses the `LayoutJoiner` algebra. Wires correctly resolve to junctions (`┬`, `┴`) and crossings (`┼`).
* **Outside the Chip (`mode_merge=False`):** The `set()` primitive defaults to standard **Overwrite**. Module boxes and external elbows are preserved exactly as drawn.

=== Surgical Routing & Track Independence
To prevent the "┼ Plague" (generic crossings replacing junctions), the engine enforces two structural rules:

1. **Independent Tracks:** Every unique signal path in a manifold is assigned its own vertical track by default, unless `share_internal_routes` is explicitly enabled.
2. **Surgical Vertical Limits:** Vertical tracks must **Stop Exactly** at their junction row.
   * **Upward Branches:** Track ends AT the source row (`intent=N`).
   * **Downward Branches:** Track ends AT the destination row (`intent=S`).
   If a track over-runs the junction by even one row, the algebra sees a "Pass-Through" (`N|S`) and forces a crossing (`┼`).

== Geometric Optimization

=== Complexity-Aware Chip Width
The width of a chip (`node.ow`) is a function of its internal manifold complexity. It scales with the number of unique vertical tracks required to resolve the `internal_wiring` without collisions.

=== Snug Bounding (Reactive Piercing)
Module boxes use **Snug Bounding**, only expanding to include vertical channels if they lead to a child within the same module. Piercing of module walls is **Reactive**: markers like `╫` and `╪` are only rendered when a wire-drawing primitive physically crosses a double-line boundary.
