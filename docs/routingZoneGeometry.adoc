== Overview

A `RoutingZone` is a rectangular world region subdivided into exactly 20 named
bands plus a central routing crossbar.  The bands are arranged as four sets of
five concentric strips, one set per cardinal side.  Every band carries a
cardinal side qualifier (`North`, `South`, `East`, `West`); no band is
anonymous.

The zone is best understood as a set of nested rectangles.  Each rectangle
peels away one band per side, revealing the next inner rectangle until only the
central crossbar remains.

== Band Ordering — Outside In

Each side contributes five bands.  The outermost band sits *outside* the zone's
bounding box (it is not enclosed by the outer `+---+` border).  The innermost
band borders the central crossbar.

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
|Order |Name |Role

|1 (outermost)
|`{side}/inter_routing_{axis}`
|Long-haul inter-zone travel band.  For North/South sides this is a
*latitude* band (runs horizontally).  For East/West sides this is a
*longitude* band (runs vertically).  Sits exterior to the zone bounding
box — routes between zones traverse this band without entering the zone
interior.

|2
|`{side}/inter_routing_fan_in_out`
|Fan-in / fan-out transition between the inter-zone travel band and the
chip terminal region.  Routes entering or leaving the zone spread or
gather here.

|3
|`{side}/chip_terminal`
|The band where placed chips live.  For a `WEST_TO_EAST` zone, chips on
the West and East sides stack vertically here; for a `NORTH_TO_SOUTH`
zone, chips on the North and South sides stack horizontally.  The band
must be tall enough (or wide enough) to accommodate all placed chips on
that side.  On sides with no placed chips the band must still exist and
must be at least one row (or column) high.

|4
|`{side}/intra_routing_fan_in_out`
|Fan-in / fan-out transition between the chip terminal region and the
intra-zone routing band.  Zone-local routes entering or leaving chip
stubs spread or gather here.

|5 (innermost)
|`{side}/intra_routing_{axis}`
|Zone-local travel band.  North/South sides: *latitude* (horizontal).
East/West sides: *longitude* (vertical).  Routes between chips within
the same zone traverse this band.

|===

The `{axis}` token is `latitude` for North/South sides and `longitude` for
East/West sides, consistent with geographic convention (latitude lines run
east-west; longitude lines run north-south).

== Central Crossbar

After all four sets of five bands are removed, the remaining rectangular
interior is the *central routing crossbar*.  It is the only area that belongs
to no single side.  It is where north/south intra-routing latitude bands and
east/west intra-routing longitude bands intersect, and where zone-internal
route corners are resolved.

== Cardinal Qualification Rule

*Every region in the zone model must carry a cardinal side qualifier.*  There
are no anonymous routing bands.  A zone in a 2D grid has independent instances
of every band on every side; without explicit qualification the model cannot
distinguish a `north/inter_routing_fan_in_out` from a
`south/inter_routing_fan_in_out`, and the solver cannot route correctly.

== Implication for the Region Model

The `RoutingZoneRegionId` type must enforce:

. `routingZoneRegionKind` is one of: `CHIP_TERMINAL`, `INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT`,
  `INTER_ROUTING_LATITUDE`, `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`, `INTRA_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT`,
  `INTRA_ROUTING_LATITUDE`, `INTRA_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
. `routingZoneRegionSide` is *never* `None` — every region carries a cardinal qualifier
. North/South regions use `LATITUDE` kinds; East/West regions use `LONGITUDE` kinds
. The `INTER_*` bands are allocated *outside* the zone bounding box in world coordinates;
  the `CHIP_TERMINAL` band is inside
. The `INTRA_*` bands are inside the chip terminal band, bordering the central crossbar

The placement solver in `routing/placement.py` must allocate bands in this order
or the geometry is wrong.

== Span Confinement Rules

Not all bands occupy their full nominal extent.  Two confinement rules govern
which bands are *full-span* and which are *corridor-only*.

=== Full-span vs corridor-only (WTE zones)

In a `WEST_TO_EAST` zone the orthogonal axis is vertical:

[cols="2,3,1"]
|===
|Band |Rule |Rows occupied

|`west/inter_routing_longitude` +
`east/inter_routing_longitude`
|**Full height** — these are the inter-zone pillar columns.  A route from an
adjacent zone arrives and departs through these columns.
|`0..H-1`

|All other vertical bands +
(inter_fan, chip_terminal, intra_fan, intra_long — both sides)
|**Crossbar rows only** — they must not bleed into the latitude band rows at
the top and bottom of the zone.
|`5..4+C`

|`north/inter_routing_latitude` +
`south/inter_routing_latitude`
|**Full width** — these are the inter-zone seam rows.
|`0` and `9+C`

|All other horizontal bands +
(inter_fan, chip_terminal, intra_fan, intra_lat — both sides)
|**Crossbar corridor only** — they span only the blank K-wide transit corridor
between the west and east chip terminal columns.  This keeps N/S routing bands
symmetrically aligned with E/W chip placement, and preserves the
pillar columns as pure through-traffic space.
|cols `4+W..3+W+K`

|===

`C = zoneVerticalSpan - 10` (chip-stack height).
`W` = west chip terminal width.  `K` = crossbar corridor width (see below).

=== Full-span vs corridor-only (NTS zones)

In a `NORTH_TO_SOUTH` zone the same rules apply on the transposed axes:

* `west/inter_routing_longitude` and `east/inter_routing_longitude` —
  **full height** (pillar columns).
* All other W/E vertical bands — **crossbar corridor rows only**
  (`N+4..N+3+K`), where `N` is the north chip terminal height.
* `north/inter_routing_latitude` and `south/inter_routing_latitude` —
  **full width** (seam rows).
* All other N/S horizontal bands — **chip-area width only**
  (cols `5..4+CW`), where `CW` is the chip width.

== Crossbar Corridor Sizing

The central blank corridor (the space that separates the W and E chip
terminal columns in a WTE zone, or the N and S chip terminal rows in a NTS
zone) has a minimum guaranteed width/height:

----
K = max(_CROSSBAR_SPAN_MIN, startChipCount, endChipCount)
_CROSSBAR_SPAN_MIN = 10
----

where `startChipCount` and `endChipCount` are the number of chips placed on
the start and end terminal sides respectively.  This ensures there is always
at least 10 units of unallocated routing space for wires to transit through
the zone regardless of chip count.

== WTE Zone Placement Formulas

Given:
* `W` = max width of any west chip (from `chipDrawLines_build`)
* `E` = max width of any east chip
* `K` = crossbar corridor width (see above)
* `C` = sum of chip heights on the taller side (chip-stack height ≥ 1)

[cols="1,2"]
|===
|Quantity |Formula

|`zoneHorizontalSpan` | `W + E + K + 8`
|`zoneVerticalSpan`   | `C + 10`
|Crossbar col start (`X`) | `4 + W`
|Crossbar col end         | `3 + W + K`
|INTER_LONG/EAST col      | `7 + W + K + E`

|===

Vertical band column positions (local, zero-based):

[cols="1,1"]
|===
|Band |Local col

|`INTER_LONG/WEST`  | `0`
|`INTER_FAN/WEST`   | `1`
|`CHIP_TERM/WEST`   | `2`
|`INTRA_FAN/WEST`   | `2 + W`
|`INTRA_LONG/WEST`  | `3 + W`
|_(blank corridor)_ | `4+W .. 3+W+K`
|`INTRA_LONG/EAST`  | `4 + W + K`
|`INTRA_FAN/EAST`   | `5 + W + K`
|`CHIP_TERM/EAST`   | `6 + W + K`
|`INTER_FAN/EAST`   | `6 + W + K + E`
|`INTER_LONG/EAST`  | `7 + W + K + E`

|===

== NTS Zone Placement Formulas

Given:
* `N` = sum of north chip heights
* `So` = sum of south chip heights
* `K` = crossbar corridor height
* `CW` = max chip width across all chips

[cols="1,2"]
|===
|Quantity |Formula

|`zoneHorizontalSpan` | `CW + 10`
|`zoneVerticalSpan`   | `N + So + K + 8`
|Crossbar row start (`Y`) | `N + 4`
|Crossbar row end         | `N + 3 + K`
|INTER_LAT/SOUTH row      | `N + So + K + 7`

|===

== Channel Routing Semantics

=== INTER vs INTRA — ring position, not zone boundary

The naming `INTER_ROUTING_*` and `INTRA_ROUTING_*` does *not* mean
"cross-zone" versus "same-zone".  It describes which *routing ring* the
channel belongs to:

* **INTRA ring** — the inner rectangular channel loop formed by
  `INTRA_ROUTING_LONGITUDE` (left/right columns) and
  `INTRA_ROUTING_LATITUDE` (top/bottom rows) of the crossbar.
  Serves the *primary flow direction* of the zone (W→E for WTE, N→S for NTS).

* **INTER ring** — the outer rectangular channel loop formed by
  `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE` (pillar columns) and
  `INTER_ROUTING_LATITUDE` (seam rows).
  Serves both inter-zone transitions (wires leaving/entering an adjacent zone)
  and intra-zone *back-edges* (wires that travel against the primary flow and
  need the outer ring to loop back).

A back-edge from the east chip to the west chip within the *same* zone is
geometrically identical to a signal that would exit and immediately re-enter —
it belongs on the outer ring because it must travel the long way around.

=== Forward route — top half of clockwise INTRA loop (WTE)

A forward wire (W chip → E chip) travels the *top half* of the clockwise
INTRA rectangle:

----
W-chip east face
  → INTRA_FAN/WEST    horizontal step out of chip terminal
  → INTRA_LONG/WEST   enter inner left column at chip port row
  → UP                ascend to INTRA_LAT/NORTH
  → INTRA_LAT/NORTH   bend right, travel east across crossbar top
  → INTRA_LONG/EAST   descend to destination port row
  → INTRA_FAN/EAST    horizontal step into chip terminal
  → E-chip west face
----

=== Return route — bottom half of clockwise INTRA loop (WTE)

A return wire (E chip → W chip) is *not* a back-edge.  It is the natural
continuation of the same clockwise rectangle, using the *bottom half* of the
INTRA ring:

----
E-chip west face
  → INTRA_FAN/EAST    horizontal step out of chip terminal
  → INTRA_LONG/EAST   continue downward in inner right column
  → DOWN              descend to INTRA_LAT/SOUTH
  → INTRA_LAT/SOUTH   bend left, travel west across crossbar bottom
  → INTRA_LONG/WEST   ascend to destination port row
  → INTRA_FAN/WEST    horizontal step into chip terminal
  → W-chip east face
----

For zone-local INTRA routing, forward and return wires for one call pair share
the *same lane* (same concentric rectangle), one occupying the top half and the
other the bottom half. Together they complete the full clockwise loop.

When N wires exit the same source chip the chip terminal fans out into N
explicit parallel stubs, each at a distinct row; a symmetric fan-in receives
the N lanes at the destination.

=== Back-edge route — perimeter loop via outer ring (WTE)

The outer ring is reserved for two traffic classes only:

* **Inter-zone traffic** — wires entering or leaving an adjacent zone via the outer seam corridor.
* **Architectural back-edges** — a wire where the east chip makes a new call to the west chip, distinct from returning to it. This requires the outer ring because the inner `INTRA` ring is reserved for the forward parent-to-child bundle and its paired return.

A same-zone back-edge uses the perimeter outer ring rather than reusing the inner `INTRA` rectangle. The forward signal and its paired return are separated onto opposite perimeter latitudes:

- one concrete wire uses the north perimeter arc
- the paired opposite-direction wire uses the south perimeter arc

One outer westbound arc reads:

----
E-chip east face
  → `Efe`             step out toward the outer ring
  → `Ee`              outer east longitude
  → `Se`              outer south latitude
  → `We`              outer west longitude
  → `Wfe`             step into the destination terminal
  → W-chip west face
----

=== March 2026 seam decision

The architectural rule for inter-zone seams is now also the implemented rule:

* seam lanes are allocated **per directed wire**
* lane identity must remain distinct across the full seam corridor
* the seam corridor includes:
** source-side `INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT`
** source-side `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
** the `RoutingZoneInterconnect` frame
** destination-side `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
** destination-side `INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT`
* `INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT` width is not just lane count; it must also
  materialize the breakout / tee / elbow structure that connects chip-side
  attachment neighborhoods into those lanes

Inter-zone and back-edge traffic compete for the same INTER channel capacity
and must be accounted for together by the route solver.

=== Lane allocation — concentric rectangles

Multiple wires sharing the same zone route each occupy a *distinct,
non-overlapping lane*.  Lanes are concentric rectangles: lane 0 is the
outermost, using the dedicated INTRA channel boundaries; each successive lane
shrinks by one cell on every side, using blank crossbar cells.

For a WTE zone, lane k uses (local zero-based coordinates):

[cols="1,2"]
|===
|Segment |Position

|Left column   | col `(3+W)+k`  (lane 0 = `INTRA_LONG/WEST`; k>0 = blank crossbar)
|Top row       | row `4+k`      (lane 0 = `INTRA_LAT/NORTH`; k>0 = blank crossbar)
|Right column  | col `(4+W+K)-k` (lane 0 = `INTRA_LONG/EAST`; k>0 = blank crossbar)
|Bottom row    | row `(5+C)-k`  (lane 0 = `INTRA_LAT/SOUTH`; k>0 = blank crossbar)
|===

A forward wire in lane k (top half, W→E):

----
(2+W,       r_src)   INTRA_FAN/WEST — horizontal exit from W chip
(3+W+k,     r_src)   approach to lane left column (horizontal)
(3+W+k,     4+k)     ascend to lane top row (vertical)
(4+W+K-k,   4+k)     traverse lane top rightward (horizontal)
(4+W+K-k,   r_dst)   descend to destination row (vertical)
(5+W+K,     r_dst)   approach to INTRA_FAN/EAST — enter E chip
----

The return wire in the same lane k (bottom half, E→W):

----
(5+W+K,     r_src_e)  INTRA_FAN/EAST — horizontal exit from E chip
(4+W+K-k,   r_src_e)  approach to lane right column (horizontal)
(4+W+K-k,   5+C-k)    descend to lane bottom row (vertical)
(3+W+k,     5+C-k)    traverse lane bottom leftward (horizontal)
(3+W+k,     r_dst_w)  ascend to destination row (vertical)
(2+W,       r_dst_w)  approach to INTRA_FAN/WEST — enter W chip
----

Non-overlap is guaranteed because:

* Source port rows `r_src` are distinct (fan-out gives one row per wire); the
  horizontal approach segments are therefore at different rows.
* Vertical ascent segments use different columns (`3+W+k` per lane).
* Horizontal top segments are at different rows (`4+k` per lane).
* Vertical descent segments use different columns (`4+W+K-k` per lane).
* Destination port rows `r_dst` are distinct (fan-in); the horizontal exit
  segments are at different rows.

Maximum lanes available: `floor(min(K, C) / 2) + 1`.
With the default K=10, C=chip-stack height ≥ 9: typically 5 or more lanes.
