== Purpose

`RoutingZone` is the atomic local routing block of the new architecture.

It is the smallest world unit that owns both chip placement and local routing.
The canonical solved unit is no longer a global chip-lane layout pass by itself.
The canonical local solved unit is now the routing zone.

Each routing zone also owns explicit geometry for its outer frame and for the
named subregions inside that frame. These subregions are not solver folklore.
They are part of the canonical model.

== Responsibilities

A routing zone owns:

- chip placement within that zone
- the solved outer frame of the zone
- explicit chip-terminal regions implied by the zone's propagation sense
- explicit intra-routing fan-in/fan-out regions
- explicit intra-routing longitude and latitude regions
- explicit inter-routing fan-in/fan-out regions
- explicit inter-routing longitude and latitude regions
- local intra-zone routing
- the local half of any traversal that reaches an interconnect seam

A routing zone does not own:

- chip identity
- world-level macro path selection
- arbitrary many-to-many junctions between zones

== Placement Rule

Chips are placed relative to the information-propagation sense of the routing
zone.

Examples:

- West-to-East sense:
  chips are placed in West/East chip-terminal regions
- North-to-South sense:
  chips are placed in North/South chip-terminal regions

No chip may appear in more than one routing zone.

== Region Rule

The region vocabulary inside a routing zone is first-class:

- `CHIP_TERMINAL`
- `INTRA_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT`
- `INTRA_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
- `INTRA_ROUTING_LATITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT`
- `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_LATITUDE`

Each owned region has explicit frame geometry. Sided regions such as chip
terminals and fan-in/fan-out bands must also record the cardinal side of the
zone on which they live.

== Model Glossary

`RoutingZoneFrame`::
The outer bounding box of one routing zone on the world canvas. This is the
total rectangular space owned by the zone.

`RoutingZoneId`::
A stable zone identity that wraps a typed address. For inter-chip world zones
the address is a `GridCoord`. For chip-internal zones the address is a
`ChipId`.

`RoutingZoneRegion`::
One explicit owned subregion inside a routing zone, such as a chip-terminal
band, an intra-routing fan-in/fan-out band, or an inter-routing longitude
channel.

`RoutingZoneRegionFrame`::
The bounding box of one `RoutingZoneRegion`. Every explicit owned region has
its own frame, and that frame must lie fully inside the owning
`RoutingZoneFrame`.

In short:

- zone frame = the whole zone
- zone id = a typed address wrapper
- region = one named typed part of the zone
- region frame = the geometry of that one named part

== Worked Example

Consider one West-to-East routing zone.

The zone itself owns one `RoutingZoneFrame`.

Inside that frame it can own, for example:

- `CHIP_TERMINAL` on the west side
- `INTRA_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT` on the west side
- `INTRA_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
- `INTRA_ROUTING_LATITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_LATITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT` on the east side
- `CHIP_TERMINAL` on the east side

Placed chips refer to the west or east `CHIP_TERMINAL` regions of that zone.
Routes then travel through the explicit fan-in/fan-out and longitude/latitude
regions until they reach the relevant boundary or interconnect seam.

== Solve Rule

Each routing zone batch-solves all of its local connectivity after:

1. chip-local geometry has been solved
2. `RoutingZoneGrid` has performed world-level capacity reservation for
   long-haul traffic

Before that final solve, the codebase now has an explicit logical placement
planning stage. That stage gives each zone a stable outer frame, explicit owned
region geometry, and ordered `ChipPlacement`s without yet claiming final route
geometry.

== Related Concepts

- `Chip`
  first-class object whose placement is owned by the zone
- `RoutingZoneInterconnect`
  continuity mediator between two neighboring zones
- `RoutingZoneGrid`
  world topology that places zones and chooses macro paths
