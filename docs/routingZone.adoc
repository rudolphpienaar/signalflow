== Purpose

`RoutingZone` is the atomic world-local routing block of the new architecture.

It is the smallest world unit that owns both chip placement and local routing.
The canonical solved unit is no longer a global chip-lane layout pass by itself.
The canonical local solved unit is now the routing zone.

Each routing zone also owns explicit geometry for its outer frame and for the
named subregions inside that frame. These subregions are not solver folklore.
They are part of the canonical model.

== Responsibilities

A routing zone owns:

- chip placement within that zone
- the solved outer frame of the zone
- explicit chip-terminal regions implied by the zone's propagation sense
- explicit intra-routing fan-in/fan-out regions
- explicit intra-routing longitude and latitude regions
- explicit inter-routing fan-in/fan-out regions
- explicit inter-routing longitude and latitude regions
- local intra-zone routing
- the local half of any traversal that reaches an interconnect seam

A routing zone does not own:

- chip identity
- world-level macro path selection
- arbitrary many-to-many junctions between zones

== Placement Rule

Chips are placed relative to the information-propagation sense of the routing
zone.

Examples:

- West-to-East sense:
  chips are placed in West/East chip-terminal regions
- North-to-South sense:
  chips are placed in North/South chip-terminal regions

No chip may appear in more than one routing zone.

== Chip Layer Rule

Every routing zone holds exactly **two chip terminal layers**: an entry-side
terminal and an exit-side terminal.

In a West-to-East zone the entry side is WEST (callers) and the exit side is
EAST (callees).  In a North-to-South zone the entry side is NORTH (callers)
and the exit side is SOUTH (callees).  The rule is sense-independent: one zone
always spans exactly one caller layer and one callee layer.

Either terminal may be empty.  When a call chain has an odd number of chips
the last occupied zone holds one chip on its entry terminal and nothing on its
exit terminal.  That is still a valid zone; its zone-local routing set is
simply empty.

Chips are assigned to zones by depth parity:

- **Even depth** (0, 2, 4, …) → entry terminal of zone `depth // 2 + 1`
- **Odd depth**  (1, 3, 5, …) → exit  terminal of zone `depth // 2 + 1`

Zone-local routing connects a zone's entry-terminal chips to its exit-terminal
chips (forward and return arcs).  Routes between the exit-terminal chips of one
zone and the entry-terminal chips of the next zone are carried by the
`RoutingZoneInterconnect` seam, not by zone-local routing.

== Region Rule

The region vocabulary inside a routing zone is first-class:

- `CHIP_TERMINAL`
- `INTRA_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT`
- `INTRA_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
- `INTRA_ROUTING_LATITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT`
- `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_LATITUDE`

Each owned region has explicit frame geometry. Sided regions such as chip
terminals and fan-in/fan-out bands must also record the cardinal side of the
zone on which they live.

== Model Glossary

`RoutingZoneFrame`::
The outer bounding box of one routing zone on the world canvas. This is the
total rectangular space owned by the zone.

`RoutingZoneId`::
A stable world-zone identity that wraps a world address. In the intended
architecture that address is a `GridCoord`. Chip-internal routing should reuse
the same routing-substrate family, but it should not rely on overloading the
world `RoutingZoneId` with chip identities.

`RoutingZoneRegion`::
One explicit owned subregion inside a routing zone, such as a chip-terminal
band, an intra-routing fan-in/fan-out band, or an inter-routing longitude
channel.

`RoutingZoneRegionFrame`::
The bounding box of one `RoutingZoneRegion`. Every explicit owned region has
its own frame, and that frame must lie fully inside the owning
`RoutingZoneFrame`.

In short:

- zone frame = the whole zone
- zone id = a typed address wrapper
- region = one named typed part of the zone
- region frame = the geometry of that one named part

== Worked Example

Consider one West-to-East routing zone.

The zone itself owns one `RoutingZoneFrame`.

Inside that frame it can own, for example:

- `CHIP_TERMINAL` on the west side
- `INTRA_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT` on the west side
- `INTRA_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
- `INTRA_ROUTING_LATITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_LATITUDE`
- `INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT` on the east side
- `CHIP_TERMINAL` on the east side

Placed chips refer to the west or east `CHIP_TERMINAL` regions of that zone.
Routes then travel through the explicit fan-in/fan-out and longitude/latitude
regions until they reach the relevant boundary or interconnect seam.

== Solve Rule

Each routing zone batch-solves all of its local connectivity after:

1. chip-local geometry has been solved
2. `RoutingZoneGrid` has performed world-level capacity reservation for
   long-haul traffic

Before that final solve, the codebase now has an explicit logical placement
planning stage. That stage gives each zone a stable outer frame, explicit owned
region geometry, and ordered `ChipPlacement`s without yet claiming final route
geometry.

The current codebase has now moved one step beyond that planning-only stage:

- `ZONE_LOCAL` call obligations are solved against the actual planned
  `RoutingZoneRegionFrame` geometry
- the solver emits explicit world-coordinate route polylines inside one zone
- those routes are still planning-grid geometry, not final ASCII projection

So a `RoutingZone` is no longer only a planned container in the new engine. It
is now also the owner of the first explicit solved inter-chip route geometry at
the local zone level.

== Relationship To Chip-Internal Routing

`RoutingZone` should not be read as the one and only routing owner in the whole
system. It is the world-level owner.

Chip-internal routing is expected to converge toward the same
routing-substrate family:

- explicit region geometry
- fan-in / fan-out transitions
- side-qualified travel bands
- one-wire-per-lane occupancy
- orthogonal route solving over named surfaces

But the owner above that substrate is different. A world routing zone owns chip
placement. A chip-internal routing owner would own chip terminals and explicit
`internal_wiring` continuity inside one chip body.

So the architectural direction is:

- keep `RoutingZone` world-scoped
- extract shared routing-substrate concepts beneath it
- let chip-internal routing reuse that substrate family through a distinct
  owner

== Geometry Dependency

The most important geometry dependency is now explicit:

1. canonical chip render geometry is solved first (`chipDrawLines_build`)
2. that chip geometry sizes the containing `RoutingZone`
3. zone-local routing geometry is solved inside that sized zone

This means chip rendering is not downstream decoration. If a chip body needs
more rows for port faces, separator rows, manifold routing, or wall-piercing
attachment language, then the containing zone must grow accordingly.

=== Intra-zone solution stability

Once a zone is locally solved its intra-zone routing solution is *immutable*.
Chip positions, crossbar width K, latitude channel rows, and all route
polylines are derived entirely from chip geometry local to that zone.  Nothing
inside the intra-zone solution references any sibling zone.

This is a deliberate design invariant: **local solutions must remain valid
under grid normalization without re-solve**.

=== Slack absorption by INTER channels

`RoutingZoneGrid` normalizes all zones in the same column to the same width
and all zones in the same row to the same height.  When a zone is smaller than
the normalized size the slack is absorbed entirely by widening the INTER-zone
channels — the `INTER_ROUTING_LONGITUDE` pillar columns and
`INTER_ROUTING_FAN_IN_OUT` bands that form the zone's outer boundary.

The intra-zone geometry (chip terminal regions, crossbar, INTRA latitude
channels) does not move.  Its world-coordinate origin shifts as the zone's
frame origin shifts, but all *relative* positions within the zone are
unchanged.  No cascade re-solve of intra-zone routes is required.

=== INTER/INTRA dead zone as guard band

The INTER latitude channels (rows 0 and 9+C in the WTE vertical layout) are
pinned to the zone boundary and never move inward.  The INTRA latitude
channels float toward chip port rows via centroid anchoring.  The space between
them — rows 1..LN-1 on the north side, rows LS+1..8+C on the south side — is
an intentional dead zone.

This dead zone is the safety buffer that prevents inter-zone through-traffic
from overlapping chip content.  Chips occupy rows 5..4+C.  The INTER channels
at rows 0 and 9+C are separated from the chip area by at least 5 rows
regardless of centroid position.

Grid normalization widens INTER channels outward, increasing the dead zone.
This makes larger grids *safer*, not more constrained.

=== Current state

The current implementation still derives zone sizes from a provisional
terminal-count formula rather than from real `chipDrawLines_build` geometry.
That should be treated as an intermediate state, not as the final
architecture.  The slack-absorption model described above is the target
design; the provisional sizing does not yet implement it.

== Related Concepts

- `Chip`
  first-class object whose placement is owned by the zone
- `RoutingZoneInterconnect`
  continuity mediator between two neighboring zones
- `RoutingZoneGrid`
  world topology that places zones and chooses macro paths
- chip-internal routing substrate
  future sibling owner over the same routing-substrate family, not a world
  zone inside the grid
